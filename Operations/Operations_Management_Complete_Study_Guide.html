1. Introduction to Operations Management

What is Operations Management?

Definition: OM is the strategic management of processes and resources that transform inputs into desired outputs. It optimizes efficiency, quality, and customer satisfaction.

Key Objectives of OM

1. Efficiency

Maximize resource utilization and minimize costs

2. Quality

Deliver products/services meeting customer expectations

3. Effectiveness

Producing right goods/services satisfying customer needs

4. Lead Time

Minimizing throughput time by reducing delays

5. Capacity Utilization

Maximizing utilization of manpower, machines

6. Cost Minimization

Reducing cost of production/service delivery

Scope of OM

  • Plant Location Facilities Layout
  • Material Handling Product Design
  • Process Design Production Planning & Control
  • Quality Control Materials Management
  • Maintenance Management

Competitive Advantages

Strategy Description
Cost Leadership Cost-efficient processes for competitive prices without compromising quality
Differentiation Unique value propositions through innovative processes and customization
Speed Efficient and agile operations to meet customer demands faster

2. Plant Location

Definition

Plant Location: Selection of specific site for establishment of the physical unit of production process. It is a strategic decision of an organization.

Need for Plant Location

New Factory
  • First-time establishment
  • Strategic expansion
Existing Factory
  • Change in resource availability
  • Shift of demand
  • New target market
  • New technology
  • Socio-political changes

Location Decision Factors

Country Level Decisions

  • Political risks, government rules, attitudes
  • Cultural and economic issues
  • Labor availability, productivity, costs
  • Availability of supplies, communications, energy
  • Exchange rates and currency risks

Region/Community Level

  • Labor availability, costs, union attitudes
  • Costs and availability of utilities
  • Environmental regulations
  • Government incentives and fiscal policies
  • Proximity to raw materials and customers

Site Level Decisions

  • Site size and cost
  • Transportation systems (air, rail, highways, water)
  • Zoning restrictions
  • Environmental impact issues

General Location Factors

Controllable Factors Uncontrollable Factors
Proximity to market Government policy
Supply of raw materials Climate conditions
Transportation facilities Supporting industries
Infrastructure availability Community attitudes
Availability of labor Community infrastructure

Location Models

1. Factor Rating Method

Identify important location factors
Rate each factor by relative importance
Assign scores to each location for each factor
Calculate rating = Factor Weight x Location Score
Select location with highest total score

2. Weighted Factor Rating Method

Weighted Score = Sum of (Weight x Score) for each factor

3. Break-even Analysis

BEP = F / (S - V)
F = Fixed Cost, S = Selling Price, V = Variable Cost per unit
Location X: Fixed Cost Rs.150,000, Variable Cost Rs.10
Location Y: Fixed Cost Rs.350,000, Variable Cost Rs.8
Crossover: 10X + 150,000 = 8X + 350,000 => X = 100,000 units
For demand 130,000 units, Location Y is appropriate.

4. Centre of Gravity Method

Cx = Sum(Dix × Wi) / Sum(Wi)
Cy = Sum(Diy × Wi) / Sum(Wi)
Dix, Diy = Coordinates; Wi = Weight/Volume

3. Plant Layout

Definition & Objectives

Plant Layout: Arrangement of machinery, equipment, and other industrial facilities for achieving quick production at least cost.

Objectives of Good Layout

Minimize
  • Material handling cost
  • Movement of people/material
  • Hazards & accidents
Maximize
  • Production capacity
  • Labor efficiency
  • Employee morale
  • Space utilization

Types of Layout

1. Product Layout (Line Layout)

Machines arranged in ONE LINE depending on sequence of operations. Materials fed in first machine, finished product from last.
Sugarcane Mill, Paper Mill, Automobiles, Chemicals, Refineries
Advantages
  • Low material handling cost
  • Less work-in-progress
  • Less skill required
  • Continuous flow
  • Shorter processing time
Disadvantages
  • High capital investment
  • One breakdown affects all
  • Slow machine affects all
  • Less flexibility

2. Process Layout (Functional Layout)

Grouping of like machines in one department. Uses general purpose machines.
Advantages
  • Breakdown doesn't affect overall productivity
  • High machine utilization
  • Flexible to design/volume changes
  • Variety of products possible
Disadvantages
  • High material handling cost
  • More time lag
  • High WIP inventory
  • More inspection required

3. Fixed Position Layout

Movement of men and machines TO the product. Used for bulky products.
Aircraft Assembly, Ship Building, Building Construction, Dams

4. Cellular Manufacturing (Group Technology)

Similar parts grouped to take advantage of similarities in design and production. Machines grouped into cells, each producing a part family.
When automated, cellular manufacturing becomes Flexible Manufacturing System (FMS)

5. Combined Layout

Combination of process and product layout. Useful when items made in different types and sizes.

Soap manufacturing: Product layout for soap production, Process layout for ancillary services (heating, glycerin, power house)

Factors Influencing Layout

  • Product: Heavy/light, big/small, liquid/solid
  • Materials: Types, storage needs, movement requirements
  • Manpower: Availability, skills, safety
  • Factory Building: Size, air conditioning, dust control
  • Location: Topography, climate, utilities
  • Management Policies: Volume, marketing, purchasing

Tools for Layout Planning

Tool Purpose
Operation Process Chart Shows points where materials introduced, sequence of operations, inspections
Flow Process Chart Graphic representation of all production activities including transport, storage, delay
Flow Diagram Represents relative position of machinery, gangways, and paths
Scale Models 3D models of wood/plastic/metal representing real situation

4. Work Study

Components of Work Study

Work Study = Method Study + Time Study
Purpose: To improve productivity by analyzing and optimizing work methods and measuring time required.

Method Study

Systematic recording and critical examination of existing and proposed ways of doing work to develop and apply easier, more effective methods.

Steps of Method Study

SELECT - Choose work to be studied
RECORD - Collect all relevant facts
EXAMINE - Critically analyze facts
DEVELOP - Develop most practical method
EVALUATE - Assess alternative solutions
DEFINE - Define new method clearly
INSTALL - Implement new method
MAINTAIN - Check regularly

Time Study

Technique of establishing an allowed time standard for a qualified worker to perform a specified task at defined level of performance.

Steps of Time Study

Select the job to be studied
Record all relevant data
Examine the method critically
Measure time (stopwatch)
Compile data and calculate standard time

Time Study Calculations

Basic Time = Observed Time × Rating/100

Standard Time = Basic Time + Allowances

Standard Time = Basic Time × (1 + Allowance%)

Types of Allowances

Allowance Type Purpose
Personal Allowance For personal needs (drinking, washroom)
Fatigue Allowance For recovery from physical/mental fatigue
Contingency Allowance For unexpected delays
Special Allowance Startup, shutdown, cleaning allowance
Rework Allowance For reworking spoiled parts
Observed time = 80 seconds, Performance rating = 80%
Basic Time = 80 × 0.80 = 64 seconds
If allowance = 15%, Standard Time = 64 × 1.15 = 73.6 seconds

5. Value Engineering / Value Analysis

Definition

Value Engineering: Systematic method to improve the "value" of goods by examining function. It analyzes products/services to achieve essential functions at lowest cost without sacrificing quality, reliability, or performance.
Value = Function / Cost
Higher value when same function at lower cost OR better function at same cost

Steps of Value Engineering

Information Phase: Gather data about product, function, cost
Analysis Phase: Analyze functions - What does it do? What should it do?
Creative Phase: Generate alternative ideas (Brainstorming)
Evaluation Phase: Evaluate alternatives for feasibility
Development Phase: Develop selected alternatives into workable solutions
Presentation Phase: Present recommendations with cost-benefit analysis
Implementation Phase: Implement approved changes

Importance of Brainstorming

Brainstorming is crucial in Value Engineering for generating creative ideas without criticism. Rules: No criticism, wild ideas welcome, quantity wanted, combination and improvement sought.

7. Material Requirements Planning (MRP)

Definition

MRP: Set of techniques using bill of materials, inventory data, and master production schedule to calculate requirements of materials. Determines WHAT to order, HOW MUCH to order, WHEN to order, WHEN to schedule delivery.

Objectives of MRP

  1. Determine Requirements: What components are needed to meet MPS
  2. Keep Priorities Current: Add, delete, expedite, delay, change orders

Inputs to MRP

1. Master Production Schedule (MPS)
  • Statement of end items to be produced
  • Quantity of each
  • Completion dates
  • Based on sales forecast
2. Bill of Materials (BOM)
  • Structure of product
  • Part numbers, descriptions
  • Quantity required
  • Material specifications
3. Inventory Records
  • Planning factors (order qty, lead times)
  • Status (on-hand, on-order, allocated)
  • Safety stock levels

MRP Terminology

Term Meaning
Dependent Demand Product demand related to other items (component of another product)
Lumpy Demand Sudden/large quantity demand to make batch of final product
Lead Time Time from order to receipt (ordering or manufacturing)
Gross Requirement Total quantity needed
Net Requirement Gross - (On hand - Safety stock - Allocated)

How MRP Works

Bucketing: Consolidate period requirements
Part Explosion: Divide items into constituent materials
Offsetting: Displace requirement by lead time
Aggregation: Find gross requirements
Netting: Calculate net requirements
Lot Sizing: Consolidate into economic order quantity

MRP Outputs

Primary Outputs
  • Planned order schedule
  • Changes in planned orders
Secondary Outputs
  • Planning reports
  • Performance reports
  • Exception reports

Benefits & Disadvantages

Benefits
  • Materials available when needed
  • Minimized inventory levels
  • Reduced customer lead times
  • Increased manufacturing efficiency
  • Better capacity utilization
Disadvantages
  • Expensive to implement
  • Lack of flexibility
  • Dependent on accurate inputs
  • Inaccuracies cause wrong estimates

8. Inventory Control

What is Inventory?

Inventory: Stock of items kept to meet future demand.
Inventory Control: Planned approach of determining what to order, when to order, how much to order, and how much to stock.

Components of Inventory

  • Raw materials
  • Purchased parts
  • Work-in-progress
  • Items being transported
  • Tools and equipment

Need for Inventory

  • Manage Bullwhip Effect (demand distortion)
  • Handle seasonal/cyclical demand
  • Provide independence from vendors
  • Take advantage of price discounts
  • Avoid work stoppages

ABC Analysis

Classification based on cost and volume - annual usage value
Class % of Items % of Value Control
A 5-15% 70-80% Tight control, accurate records, frequent review
B 30% 15% Normal control, periodic review
C 50-60% 5-10% Simple control, large orders
ABC Analysis helps manager focus attention on few important items (A class) and show results quickly.

VED Analysis

Classification based on criticality to production
Category Description
V - Vital Unavailability will STOP production
E - Essential Stock-out costs are VERY HIGH
D - Desirable No immediate stoppage, nominal stock-out costs

Economic Order Quantity (EOQ)

EOQ: Optimal order quantity that minimizes total inventory cost

Assumptions

  • Demand is known and constant
  • No shortages allowed
  • Lead time is constant
  • Order received all at once
EOQ = √(2 × D × Co / Cc)

D = Annual Demand
Co = Ordering Cost per order
Cc = Annual Carrying Cost per unit
Total Cost = (Co × D/Q) + (Cc × Q/2)
Ordering Cost + Carrying Cost

9. Sequencing

Definition

Sequencing: Determining the order in which jobs are processed on machines to minimize total elapsed time, idle time, and optimize resource utilization.

Johnson's Rule (2 Machines)

Used to find optimal sequence for n jobs on 2 machines (A & B) in order AB.

Steps

List all jobs with processing times on both machines
Find minimum processing time among all jobs
If minimum is on Machine A: Place job FIRST in sequence
If minimum is on Machine B: Place job LAST in sequence
Remove job from list and repeat until all jobs sequenced
Jobs: J1(A=2,B=3), J2(A=4,B=6), J3(A=5,B=1), J4(A=7,B=4), J5(A=1,B=8)
Min = 1 (J5 on A) → Place J5 first
Min = 1 (J3 on B) → Place J3 last
Continue... Optimal: J5-J1-J2-J4-J3

Extended Johnson's Rule (3+ Machines)

Can be applied when: Min time on Machine 1 ≥ Max time on Machine 2
OR Min time on Machine 3 ≥ Max time on Machine 2

Method

Convert to 2-machine problem:

  • Machine G = Machine 1 + Machine 2 times
  • Machine H = Machine 2 + Machine 3 times
  • Apply Johnson's rule on G and H

Graphical Method (2 Jobs, Multiple Machines)

Used when 2 jobs need to be processed on multiple machines with different sequences.

Draw axes with Job 1 times on X-axis, Job 2 times on Y-axis
Mark blocks for each machine (one job at a time)
Find path that avoids all blocked regions
Calculate total elapsed time

10. Supply Chain Management

What is Supply Chain?

Supply Chain: All stages involved directly/indirectly in fulfilling customer request. Includes manufacturers, suppliers, transporters, warehouses, retailers, and customers.

Flows in Supply Chain

  • Information Flow: Capacity, schedules, orders, quality data
  • Material Flow: Raw materials → WIP → Finished goods
  • Finance Flow: Credits, payments, invoices

Types of Supply Chains

Type Example
Manufacturer → Wholesaler → Retailer → Customer Traditional FMCG
Manufacturer → Retailer → Customer Big Bazaar, Reliance
Manufacturer → Customer BHEL

SCM Objective

Maximize Supply Chain Value
Value = What product is worth to customer - Effort to fill request
Profitability = Revenue - Total SC Cost

Supplier Tiers

  • Tier 1: Directly supplies to firm doing business with final customer
  • Tier 2: Supplies to Tier 1 suppliers
  • Tier 3: Supplies to Tier 2 suppliers

Bullwhip Effect

Bullwhip Effect: Demand information gets distorted as it moves away from end customer. Leads to higher safety stock inventories.

Ways to Reduce Bullwhip Effect

  • Good demand forecasting
  • Information sharing (EDI)
  • Coordination among partners
  • Eliminate price promotions/quantity discounts
  • Use bar codes and electronic scanning

Key SCM Concepts

Vertical Integration

  • Backward: Acquiring control of raw material suppliers
  • Forward: Acquiring control of distribution channels

Outsourcing

Paying third-party suppliers for raw materials/services instead of in-house production.

Case Studies

Coca-Cola Success

  • 225 bottling partners worldwide
  • 1.9 billion servings daily in 200+ countries
  • Local sourcing (water, sugar)
  • Blockchain for partner coordination
  • Recycled marine plastic bottles

McDonald's Success

  • 3-Legged Stool: Franchisees, Suppliers, Employees
  • Long-term supplier relationships (Martin-Brower since 1956)
  • Effective vertical integration
  • Controls meat processing, potato growing, transportation

Supply Chain Failures

  • Boeing 787: Outsourced core functions to 50+ suppliers, quality issues, 3-year delay
  • KFC UK 2018: Chicken shortage, DHL had no chilled food experience, single distribution center, 700 stores closed

11. Statistical Quality Control (SQC)

Definition

Quality: Fitness for purpose at lowest cost. Degree to which product fulfills customer requirements.

SQC: Quality control system using statistical techniques to control, improve, and maintain quality through inspection, testing, and analysis.

Factors Affecting Quality

5 M's: Men, Materials, Machines, Manufacturing conditions, Money + Management, Market Research

Objectives of Quality Control

  • Decide quality standards acceptable to customers
  • Take measures to improve quality standards
  • Solve deviations during manufacturing

Divisions of SQC

Category Purpose
Descriptive Statistics Describe quality characteristics (Mean, SD, Range)
SPC (Statistical Process Control) Inspect random samples, decide if process within range
Acceptance Sampling Randomly inspect samples, accept/reject entire lot

Causes of Variation

Chance Causes (Common/Random)
  • Natural/inherent variation
  • Aged machines, material variability
  • Poor lighting, worker training
  • Management responsibility
Assignable Causes (Special/Non-random)
  • Due to change in material, machine, operator
  • Machine improper setting
  • Defective raw material batch
  • Worker/supervisor responsibility

Control Charts

Control Charts: Graphical representation based on statistical sampling theory to detect variations and warn of departure from tolerance limits.

Components of Control Chart

  • Centre Line (CL): Average value or standard value (solid line)
  • Upper Control Limit (UCL): Upper value of variation (dotted line)
  • Lower Control Limit (LCL): Lower value of variation (dotted line)
3-Sigma Limits
UCL = Mean + 3σ
LCL = Mean - 3σ
Data beyond 3σ represents less than 1% of all points

Types of Control Charts

For Variables (Measurable) For Attributes (Count)
X-bar Chart (Mean) p-Chart (Proportion defective)
R Chart (Range) np-Chart (Number defective)
σ Chart (Std Dev) c-Chart (Defects per unit)

Advantages of Control Charts

  • Indicates if process in control or out of control
  • Determines process variability
  • Ensures product quality level
  • Warns in time, reduces scrap/rejection
  • Builds organization reputation

12. Lean Management & Theory of Constraints

Lean Management

Lean Management: Customer-focused waste elimination method that makes process and organization lean. Long-term approach seeking small, incremental changes to improve efficiency and quality.

The 3 M's of Lean

Term Meaning Focus
Muda Waste Activities to be eliminated
Mura Inconsistency Increases variability
Muri Unreasonableness Blaming vs resolving

7 Types of Waste (Muda)

  1. Excess/early production
  2. Delays
  3. Movement and transport
  4. Poor process design
  5. Inventory
  6. Inefficient performance
  7. Making defective items

The 5 S's of Lean

Japanese English Meaning
Seiri Sort Keep necessary items only
Seiton Set-in-order Efficient placement
Seiso Shine/Sweep Cleanliness
Seiketsu Standardize Continuous improvement
Shitsuke Sustain Discipline

5 Steps of Lean Implementation

Specify Value: Define value from customer perspective
Map: Identify value stream, create current/future state map
Flow: Make remaining steps flow, eliminate functional barriers
Pull: Let customer pull products, eliminate need for forecast
Perfection: Continuous improvement, return to step 1

Theory of Constraints (TOC)

TOC: Methodology for identifying most important limiting factor (constraint/bottleneck) and systematically improving it. Every complex system has one constraint that limits the entire system.

Developed by Dr. Eliyahu Goldratt (1984 book "The Goal")

Five Focusing Steps of TOC

IDENTIFY the system's constraint
EXPLOIT the constraint (maximize output)
SUBORDINATE everything else to the constraint
ELEVATE the constraint (add capacity)
REPEAT - Don't let inertia become constraint

Throughput Accounting

Alternative to traditional accounting. Considers inventory as LIABILITY (not asset).
Net Profit = Throughput - Operating Expenses
ROI = Net Profit / Investment
Productivity = Throughput / Operating Expenses
Investment Turns = Throughput / Investment

TOC Priority (in order)

  1. Increase Throughput (highest priority)
  2. Reduce Investment
  3. Reduce Operating Expenses

Benefits of TOC

  • Increased profit
  • Fast improvement
  • Improved capacity
  • Reduced lead times
  • Reduced inventory

13. Demand Forecasting

Definition

Demand Forecasting: Process of predicting future demand for products/services using historical data and various techniques.

Types of Forecasting Methods

1. Qualitative Methods

Method Description
Delphi Method Expert panel gives anonymous opinions, iteratively refined
Market Research Surveys, interviews, focus groups
Sales Force Composite Sales team estimates compiled
Executive Opinion Management judgment combined

2. Quantitative Methods

Method Description
Moving Average Average of last n periods
Weighted Moving Average Recent periods given more weight
Exponential Smoothing Weighted average with smoothing constant α
Linear Regression Trend line based on historical data

Key Formulas

Simple Moving Average
MA = (D₁ + D₂ + ... + Dₙ) / n
Exponential Smoothing
Ft = Ft-1 + α(At-1 - Ft-1)
Ft = Forecast, At-1 = Actual last period, α = Smoothing constant (0-1)
Forecast Error Measures
MAD = Σ|Actual - Forecast| / n
MSE = Σ(Actual - Forecast)² / n
MAPE = (Σ|Error|/Actual) × 100 / n

Factors Affecting Forecasting

  • Historical data availability and quality
  • Time horizon (short/medium/long term)
  • Market conditions and competition
  • Economic factors
  • Seasonal patterns
  • Product life cycle stage
