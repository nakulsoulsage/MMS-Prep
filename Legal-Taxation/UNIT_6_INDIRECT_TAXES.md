## 6.1 GOODS AND SERVICES TAX (GST)

### 6.1.1 Introduction to GST

#### Background:
- **Implemented**: July 1, 2017
- **"One Nation, One Tax"**: Unified indirect tax system
- **Replaced** 17 central and state taxes:
  - Central Excise Duty
  - Service Tax
  - VAT
  - Central Sales Tax
  - Luxury Tax
  - Entertainment Tax
  - Entry Tax
  - Octroi
  - And others

#### Constitutional Amendment:
- **101st Constitution Amendment Act, 2016**
- **Article 246A**: GST on goods and services
- **Article 279A**: GST Council

---

### 6.1.2 GST Council (Article 279A)

**Composition**:
- **Chairperson**: Union Finance Minister
- **Members**:
  - Union Minister of State (Finance)
  - State Finance Ministers of all states/UTs

**Functions**:
1. Recommend GST rates, exemptions, thresholds
2. Model GST laws
3. Special provisions for states
4. Dispute resolution mechanism

**Voting**:
- Centre: **1/3rd** voting power
- States: **2/3rd** voting power (collectively)
- **3/4th majority** required for decisions

---

### 6.1.3 Types of GST

#### 1. CGST (Central GST):
- Levied by **Central Government**
- On **intra-state** supply
- Example: Supply within Maharashtra - CGST + SGST

#### 2. SGST (State GST):
- Levied by **State Government**
- On **intra-state** supply
- Example: Supply within Maharashtra - CGST + SGST

#### 3. IGST (Integrated GST):
- Levied by **Central Government**
- On **inter-state** supply and **imports**
- Example: Supply from Maharashtra to Gujarat - IGST
- IGST = CGST + SGST

#### 4. UTGST (Union Territory GST):
- Levied by **Union Territory Government**
- On **intra-UT** supply
- Applicable to UTs without legislature

---

### 6.1.4 Key Definitions

#### Supply (Section 7, CGST Act):
"Supply includes:
1. All forms of supply of goods or services or both
2. Sale, transfer, barter, exchange, license, rental, lease, disposal
3. Import of services for consideration
4. Activities specified in Schedule I (even without consideration)
5. Activities to be treated as supply of goods or services as per Schedule II"

**Inclusive Definition**: Very broad, covers almost all transactions

---

#### Goods (Section 2(52)):
"Every kind of movable property other than money and securities"
- Includes growing crops, grass, things attached to earth

---

#### Services (Section 2(102)):
"Anything other than goods, money and securities"
- Includes activities, intangible property
- Negative definition

---

#### Taxable Person (Section 2(107)):
"Person registered or liable to be registered under GST Act"

---

#### Aggregate Turnover (Section 2(6)):
"Aggregate value of:
- All taxable supplies
- Exempt supplies
- Exports of goods/services
- Inter-state supplies

Of person having same PAN"

**Excludes**:
- Taxes (CGST, SGST, IGST, Cess)
- Value of inward supplies (reverse charge basis)

---

### 6.1.5 Registration (Sections 22-30)

#### Compulsory Registration (Section 22):

**Threshold Limits**:
1. **Normal States**: Aggregate turnover > **₹40 lakh** (goods) or **₹20 lakh** (services)
2. **Special Category States** (NE states, Himachal, Uttarakhand): > **₹20 lakh** (goods) or **₹10 lakh** (services)

**Exceptions** (Compulsory regardless of turnover):
1. Inter-state supply
2. E-commerce operators
3. Casual taxable persons
4. Non-resident taxable persons
5. Input Service Distributors
6. Reverse charge mechanism suppliers
7. E-commerce aggregators
8. Persons paying tax under composition scheme (if turnover exceeds)

---

#### Voluntary Registration:
- Even if turnover below threshold
- To claim input tax credit
- To supply to registered persons

---

#### Procedure:
1. Apply online on GST portal
2. **Form GST REG-01**
3. Documents:
   - PAN card
   - Aadhaar card
   - Proof of business
   - Bank account details
   - Photographs
4. **ARN (Application Reference Number)** generated
5. Verification by officer
6. **GSTIN (GST Identification Number)** issued
   - **15 digits**: State code (2) + PAN (10) + Entity code (2) + Check digit (1)

---

### 6.1.6 GST Rates and Slabs

#### Tax Slabs:

1. **0% (Nil Rated)**:
   - Essential items: Fresh vegetables, fruits, milk, curd, eggs, bread
   - Services: Health care, education

2. **0.25%**:
   - Rough diamonds, precious stones

3. **3%**:
   - Gold, silver ornaments

4. **5%**:
   - Essential goods: Edible oil, tea, coffee, sugar, coal, spices
   - Medicines, life-saving drugs
   - Transport services

5. **12%**:
   - Processed food, butter, cheese, ghee
   - Computers, mobile phones
   - Business class air travel

6. **18%** (Standard Rate):
   - Most goods and services
   - IT services, telecom services
   - Banking services, financial services
   - Restaurant services (without AC)
   - Soaps, toothpaste, hair oil

7. **28%**:
   - Luxury/sin goods: Cigarettes, tobacco, aerated drinks
   - Air conditioners, automobiles (above certain CC)
   - Cement
   - 5-star hotels

**Cess**: Additional on luxury/sin goods (over and above 28%)

---

### 6.1.7 Composition Scheme (Section 10)

**Purpose**: Simpler compliance for small taxpayers

**Eligibility**:
- Aggregate turnover ≤ **₹1.5 crore** (₹75 lakh for NE/hill states)
- Not engaged in inter-state supply
- Not engaged in e-commerce supply
- Not a manufacturer of ice cream, tobacco, pan masala

**Tax Rates**:
- **Manufacturers/Traders**: **1%** of turnover (0.5% CGST + 0.5% SGST)
- **Restaurants**: **5%** of turnover (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST)
- **Service Providers**: **6%** of turnover (3% CGST + 3% SGST)

**Conditions**:
- Cannot collect GST from customers
- **No input tax credit** available
- Quarterly return (earlier annual)
- Tax calculated on turnover
- Words "composition taxable person" on signboard

---

### 6.1.8 Time and Place of Supply

#### Time of Supply of Goods (Section 12):

**Earliest of**:
1. Date of **issue of invoice**
2. Date of **receipt of payment**
3. Date on which **goods removed** for supply
4. **31st day** from date of issue of invoice by recipient (reverse charge)

---

#### Time of Supply of Services (Section 13):

**Earliest of**:
1. Date of **issue of invoice** (or last date by which invoice to be issued)
2. Date of **receipt of payment**
3. **Date of completion** of provision of service (in case of reverse charge)

---

#### Place of Supply (Sections 10-13, IGST Act):

**Determines**: Whether CGST+SGST (intra-state) or IGST (inter-state)

**Goods**:
- Where movement terminates for delivery
- If no movement: Location of goods at time of delivery

**Services**:
- Location of **recipient** of services (general rule)
- Specific rules for different services:
  - Immovable property: Location of property
  - Restaurant: Location of restaurant
  - Passenger transport: Place where passenger embarks
  - Online services: Location of recipient
  - Banking/financial services: Location of recipient

---

### 6.1.9 Taxable Value (Section 15)

**Transaction Value**: Price actually paid or payable

**Includes**:
- Any taxes, duties, fees (except GST)
- Packing charges
- Commission
- Interest for delayed payment
- Subsidies linked to price
- Incidental expenses (before/during delivery)

**Excludes**:
- Discount (before/at time of supply, as per agreement)
- GST

**Valuation Methods**:
1. **Transaction value** (usual method)
2. **Value of like goods/services**
3. **Cost + 10% method**
4. **Reasonable means**

---

### 6.1.10 Input Tax Credit (ITC) (Sections 16-21)

#### Concept:
- Tax paid on **inputs** can be claimed as credit
- Set off against **output tax liability**
- Prevents cascading effect (tax on tax)

---

#### Conditions for ITC (Section 16):

1. **Possession of tax invoice** or debit note
2. **Goods/services received**
3. **Tax paid** to government by supplier
4. **Return filed** by recipient
5. **Goods/services used** for business purposes

---

#### ITC Not Available (Section 17(5)):

1. **Motor vehicles** (except for specific purposes)
2. **Food, beverages** (outdoor catering)
3. **Membership of club**, **health and fitness center**
4. **Rent-a-cab** (except specified)
5. **Works contract** for immovable property (except plant & machinery)
6. **Goods lost/destroyed/written off**
7. **Personal use goods/services**
8. Goods/services on which GST paid under **composition scheme**

---

#### Reversal of ITC:
- If goods/services used for **exempt supplies**
- If goods/services used for **personal purposes**
- If **payment not made** to supplier within **180 days**
- If ITC wrongly claimed

---

#### Input Service Distributor (ISD):
- Office of supplier having same PAN
- Distributes ITC to other branches/units
- Issues ISD invoice

---

### 6.1.11 Returns (Sections 37-45)

#### Types of Returns:

##### 1. GSTR-1 (Outward Supplies):
- **Monthly** (turnover > ₹5 crore)
- **Quarterly** (turnover ≤ ₹5 crore)
- Due date: **11th** of next month

##### 2. GSTR-2A (Auto-populated):
- Inward supplies (from supplier's GSTR-1)
- View only, not to be filed

##### 3. GSTR-2B (Auto-generated):
- ITC statement
- Static, generated on 12th of next month

##### 4. GSTR-3B (Summary Return):
- **Monthly** for all
- Summary of outward supplies, ITC claimed, tax payable
- Due date: **20th/24th** of next month
- Payment made through this

##### 5. GSTR-4 (Composition Scheme):
- **Quarterly**
- Composition dealers
- Due date: **18th** of month following quarter

##### 6. GSTR-5 (Non-Resident):
- Within **20 days** after validity of registration expires

##### 7. GSTR-6 (Input Service Distributor):
- **Monthly**
- Due date: **13th** of next month

##### 8. GSTR-7 (TDS Deductors):
- **Monthly**
- Due date: **10th** of next month

##### 9. GSTR-8 (E-commerce Operators):
- **Monthly**
- Due date: **10th** of next month

##### 10. GSTR-9 (Annual Return):
- **Annual**
- Consolidated return
- Due date: **31st December** of next FY
- Compulsory if turnover > ₹2 crore

##### 11. GSTR-9C (Audit/Reconciliation):
- For turnover > ₹5 crore
- Certified by CA/CMA
- Due date: **31st December** of next FY

---

### 6.1.12 Payment of Tax (Section 49)

#### E-Payment:
- Through GST portal
- Challan generated
- Payment through net banking, debit card, NEFT/RTGS

#### Electronic Cash Ledger:
- Money deposited in cash ledger
- Used to pay tax liability
- Can be used for tax, interest, penalty, fees

#### Electronic Credit Ledger:
- ITC reflected here
- Used to set off output tax
- **Order of utilization**:
  1. IGST credit can be used for IGST, CGST, SGST (in that order)
  2. CGST credit can be used for CGST, then IGST only
  3. SGST credit can be used for SGST, then IGST only
  4. CGST and SGST credits cannot be cross-utilized

---

### 6.1.13 Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) (Section 9(3))

**Concept**: Recipient of goods/services liable to pay tax (instead of supplier)

**Applicability**:
1. **Unregistered supplier** to **registered recipient**
   - Exemption if recipient's aggregate turnover ≤ ₹5 crore
2. **Specified services** (as notified):
   - GTA (Goods Transport Agency) services
   - Advocate services
   - Sponsorship services
3. **Imports** of services

**Procedure**:
- Recipient pays tax
- Can claim ITC (if eligible)
- Shown separately in returns

---

### 6.1.14 Tax Deduction at Source (TDS) (Section 51)

**Applicable to**:
- Central/State Government departments
- Local authorities
- Government agencies
- Notified persons/entities

**Rate**: **2%** (1% CGST + 1% SGST or 2% IGST)

**Threshold**: Payment > **₹2.5 lakh** under single contract

**Procedure**:
- Deduct TDS from payment
- Deposit with government
- Issue TDS certificate
- File GSTR-7

**Credit**: Recipient can claim ITC of TDS

---

### 6.1.15 Tax Collection at Source (TCS) (Section 52)

**Applicable to**: E-commerce operators

**Rate**: **1%** (0.5% CGST + 0.5% SGST or 1% IGST)

**On**: Net value of taxable supplies made through operator

**Threshold**: ₹5 lakh (in previous year or current FY)

**Procedure**:
- Collect TCS from supplier
- Deposit with government
- File GSTR-8

**Credit**: Supplier can claim ITC of TCS

---

### 6.1.16 Refunds (Section 54)

#### Types of Refunds:

1. **Export refund**
2. **Accumulated ITC** (inverted duty structure)
3. **Excess payment** of tax
4. **ITC on capital goods** (if registered person opts out)
5. **Finalization of provisional assessment**
6. **UN agencies/embassies**

**Time Limit**: **2 years** from relevant date

**Procedure**:
- Apply online (RFD-01)
- Documents uploaded
- Processing by officer
- Sanction order
- Refund credited to bank account

**Time for Sanction**: **60 days** from complete application

**Interest**: If refund not sanctioned within 60 days, interest @6% p.a.

---

### 6.1.17 Audit, Assessment, Investigation

#### Audit (Section 65):
- By **CA/CMA** if turnover > **₹5 crore**
- GSTR-9C filed
- Reconciliation statement

#### Assessment (Sections 59-64):

##### 1. Self-Assessment:
- By taxpayer himself in returns

##### 2. Provisional Assessment:
- When value not ascertainable
- Furnish bond/security
- Final assessment after value determined

##### 3. Scrutiny Assessment:
- Discrepancy in returns
- Notice issued
- Reply within 30 days
- Order passed

##### 4. Best Judgment Assessment:
- Non-filing of returns
- Non-cooperation in scrutiny
- Officer determines liability

#### Investigation (Sections 67-73):
- Search and seizure
- Summons
- Inspection of goods in transit
- Power to arrest (for serious offenses)
- Power to inspect

---

### 6.1.18 Penalties and Offences

#### Penalty (Section 122):

1. **₹10,000** or **tax amount** (whichever higher):
   - Supplying without invoice
   - Issuing invoice without supply
   - Collecting tax without registration
   - Taking/utilizing wrong ITC
   - Non-payment of tax collected

2. **100% of tax amount**:
   - Suppression of turnover
   - Taking/utilizing ITC without actual receipt of goods/services

3. **200% of tax amount**:
   - Supplying goods liable to confiscation

**Compounding**:
- Lesser offenses can be compounded
- Payment of compounding amount
- Criminal proceedings dropped

---

#### Offences (Section 132):

**Cognizable and Non-bailable**:
1. Tax evasion > ₹5 crore
2. Issuing invoice without supply to evade tax > ₹2 crore
3. Taking/utilizing fake ITC > ₹2 crore
4. Obstructing officer in discharge of duty

**Punishment**:
- Imprisonment up to **5 years** + fine
- If amount > ₹5 crore: **5 years**
- If amount ≤ ₹5 crore but > ₹2 crore: **3 years**
- If amount ≤ ₹2 crore: **1 year**

---

### 6.1.19 Appeals and Revision

#### Hierarchy:

**First Appeal** (Section 107):
- **To**: Appellate Authority (Commissioner Appeals)
- **Within**: **3 months** from date of order
- **Condition**: 10% of disputed tax deposited

**Second Appeal** (Section 112):
- **To**: Appellate Tribunal
- **Within**: **3 months** from date of order of First Appellate Authority
- **Condition**: 20% of disputed tax deposited

**Further Appeals**:
- **High Court**: On substantial question of law
- **Supreme Court**: Final appeal

**Revision** (Section 108):
- By Commissioner on own motion or on information
- Within **3 years** from date of order
- Revision if order prejudicial to revenue

---

### 6.1.20 GST for Business/Management

#### Importance for Managers:

##### 1. Compliance:
- Timely registration
- Accurate invoicing
- Timely filing of returns
- Proper maintenance of records

##### 2. Pricing Strategy:
- GST rate on products/services
- Input tax credit availability
- Competitive pricing

##### 3. Cash Flow Management:
- Payment of GST monthly
- ITC claims
- Working capital impact
- Refund timelines

##### 4. Supply Chain:
- Interstate vs intrastate supplies
- Place of supply implications
- E-way bill compliance
- Logistics optimization

##### 5. IT Systems:
- GST-compliant software
- E-invoicing (turnover > ₹5 crore)
- API integration with GSTN
- Automated reconciliation

##### 6. Documentation:
- Tax invoices
- E-way bills
- GST returns
- ITC documentation
- Audit trail

## 6.2 CUSTOMS ACT, 1962

### 6.2.1 Introduction to Customs

#### Background:
- **Enacted**: 1962
- **Objective**: Regulate import and export, collect customs duty, prevent smuggling
- **Administered by**: Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC)

#### Purpose:
- Generate revenue for government
- Protect domestic industry
- Regulate international trade
- Prevent smuggling and illegal imports
- Implement trade policy

---

### 6.2.2 Definitions (Section 2)

#### Baggage (Section 2(3)):
"Goods imported or exported as accompanied or unaccompanied personal effects by passenger or member of crew"

#### Bill of Entry (Section 2(4)):
"Document filed by importer for clearance of imported goods"

#### Customs Area (Section 2(11)):
"Area of a customs station, warehouse, or any area notified by Board"

#### Customs Port/Airport (Sections 2(12), 2(13)):
- Port/airport appointed for loading/unloading of goods, embarkation/disembarkation of passengers
- Notified by Central Government

#### Dutiable Goods (Section 2(14)):
"Goods on which customs duty is leviable"

#### Export (Section 2(18)):
"Taking out of goods from India to a place outside India"

#### Import (Section 2(23)):
"Bringing into India goods from a place outside India"

#### Indian Customs Waters (Section 2(28)):
"Waters extending up to territorial waters of India"
- Territorial waters: **12 nautical miles** from baseline

#### Prohibited Goods (Section 2(33)):
"Goods import or export of which is prohibited under Customs Act or any other law"

---

### 6.2.3 Types of Customs Duties

#### 1. Basic Customs Duty (BCD) (Section 12):
- Levied on **import** of goods
- Rates specified in **Customs Tariff Act, 1975**
- Based on **Harmonized System of Nomenclature (HSN)**
- Usual rates: **5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 28%** (varies by product)

**Calculation**:
BCD = Assessable Value × BCD Rate

---

#### 2. Integrated Tax (IGST):
- Levied on **import** of goods
- At same rate as domestic supplies
- Calculated on: **(Assessable Value + BCD + Social Welfare Surcharge)**

**Calculation**:
IGST = (Assessable Value + BCD + SWS) × IGST Rate

---

#### 3. Goods and Services Tax Compensation Cess:
- Additional cess on luxury/sin goods
- On imports and domestic supplies

---

#### 4. Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS):
- **10%** of BCD (earlier education cess 3%, then 4%)
- Since FY 2018-19

---

#### 5. Anti-Dumping Duty (Section 9A):
- To counter dumping of goods
- Goods exported at price lower than normal value
- Injury to domestic industry
- Duty equal to margin of dumping

---

#### 6. Safeguard Duty (Section 8B):
- To protect domestic industry from sudden surge in imports
- Temporary measure
- Due to unforeseen circumstances

---

#### 7. Countervailing Duty (CVD):
- To counter subsidies given by exporting country
- Now subsumed in IGST

---

### 6.2.4 Valuation for Customs (Sections 14, Customs Valuation Rules)

#### Transaction Value Method (Rule 3):
**General Rule**: Value = **Transaction Value**

**Transaction Value**:
- Price actually paid or payable for goods when sold for export to India
- Adjusted for following (if not included):
  1. Commission and brokerage (except buying commission)
  2. Cost of containers/packing
  3. Assists (materials, tools, engineering, provided free)
  4. Royalties and license fees
  5. Proceeds of resale accruing to seller
  6. Cost of transport, insurance (till place of importation)
  7. Loading, unloading, handling charges

**Plus**:
- Freight (till port of importation)
- Insurance
- Loading, unloading, handling charges

**Minus**:
- Discounts
- Post-import expenses (after customs clearance)

---

#### Other Valuation Methods (if transaction value not available):

**Rule 4**: Transaction value of identical goods
**Rule 5**: Transaction value of similar goods
**Rule 6**: Deductive value (based on selling price in India)
**Rule 7**: Computed value (cost of production + profit)
**Rule 8**: Residual method (reasonable means)

---

#### Valuation for Export:
- FOB (Free on Board) value
- Value of goods at port of export

---

### 6.2.5 Import Procedure

#### Step 1: Obtaining IEC (Import Export Code):
- From **DGFT (Directorate General of Foreign Trade)**
- 10-digit code
- Mandatory for importers/exporters
- One-time registration

---

#### Step 2: Advance Filing of Bill of Entry:
- **Before arrival** of goods (since 2018)
- **Bill of Entry**: Document for customs clearance
- Filed electronically on **ICEGATE** (Indian Customs EDI Gateway)
- Three types:
  1. **Into Bond Bill of Entry** (Warehousing)
  2. **Ex-Bond Bill of Entry** (Clearance from warehouse)
  3. **Home Consumption Bill of Entry** (Direct clearance)

---

#### Step 3: Arrival of Goods:
- **Import General Manifest (IGM)** filed by carrier
- Details of cargo
- Filed before arrival or within 12 hours of arrival

---

#### Step 4: Assessment:
- Customs officer assesses duty
- Verification of documents:
  - Invoice
  - Packing list
  - Bill of lading/Airway bill
  - Certificate of origin
  - Insurance document
- Examination of goods (if required)
- Duty calculated

**Types of Assessment**:
1. **Self-Assessment** (by importer)
2. **Provisional Assessment** (final value not known)
3. **Re-assessment** (error in original assessment)

---

#### Step 5: Payment of Duty:
- Online payment
- Through e-payment gateway
- Challan generated

---

#### Step 6: Out of Charge:
- After duty payment
- Order for clearance issued
- "Out of Charge" order

---

#### Step 7: Delivery of Goods:
- From customs area
- Delivery order from custodian
- Goods cleared

---

### 6.2.6 Export Procedure

#### Step 1: Shipping Bill:
- Document for export clearance
- Filed electronically on ICEGATE
- Types:
  1. **Free Shipping Bill** (duty-free exports)
  2. **Dutiable Shipping Bill** (export duty payable)
  3. **Drawback Shipping Bill** (claiming duty drawback)
  4. **DEPB Shipping Bill** (under Duty Entitlement Pass Book scheme)

---

#### Step 2: Export General Manifest (EGM):
- Filed by carrier
- Details of cargo to be exported

---

#### Step 3: Let Export Order:
- Customs clearance for export
- After examination (if required)
- "Let Export" order issued

---

#### Step 4: Shipment:
- Goods loaded on vessel/aircraft
- Mate's Receipt issued
- Bill of Lading issued (proof of shipment)

---

#### Step 5: Export Benefits:
- **Duty Drawback**: Refund of customs/excise duty paid on inputs
- **MEIS (Merchandise Exports from India Scheme)**: Duty credit scrips
- **SEIS (Service Exports from India Scheme)**: Duty credit for services
- **Export promotion schemes**

---

### 6.2.7 Warehousing (Sections 57-73)

#### Customs Warehouse:
- Place for storing imported goods without payment of duty
- Until goods cleared for home consumption
- **Public warehouse** (licensed) or **Private warehouse**

**Benefits**:
1. Deferred payment of duty
2. Re-export without paying duty
3. Processing/manufacturing allowed (in some cases)
4. Blending, repacking allowed

**Warehousing Period**:
- Initially: **1 year**
- Extension: Up to **2 years** (for sufficient reason)
- Further extension: By Commissioner

**Interest**: If duty not paid after initial period, interest @15% p.a.

---

### 6.2.8 Baggage Rules (Baggage Rules, 2016)

#### Free Allowance:

**For passengers arriving from abroad**:
- **₹50,000** (for passengers from countries other than Nepal, Bhutan, Myanmar, China)
- **₹15,000** (for passengers from Nepal, Bhutan, Myanmar, China)
- Duty-free allowance per passenger

**Infants**: No free allowance

**Used personal effects**: Duty-free (if bona fide)

**Alcohol and Tobacco**:
- Alcoholic liquors: Up to **2 liters** duty-free
- Cigarettes: **100 cigarettes** or cigars **25** or tobacco **125 grams**

**Laptop, Camera**: 1 each duty-free

---

### 6.2.9 Drawback (Sections 74-75, Drawback Rules)

**Duty Drawback**: Refund of customs/excise duty paid on inputs used in export goods

**Types**:

#### 1. All Industry Rate (AIR):
- Standard rates notified by government
- Based on product category
- Easy to claim

#### 2. Brand Rate:
- Specific to manufacturer
- Based on actual duty paid on inputs
- Needs approval from authorities

**Conditions**:
- Goods must be exported
- Duty should have been paid on inputs
- Goods manufactured/processed in India
- Claim within **2 years** of export

---

### 6.2.10 Refund (Section 27)

**Grounds for Refund**:
1. Duty paid in excess
2. Goods exported or destroyed (before clearance)
3. Duty paid on re-export
4. Provisional assessment finalized (duty less than provisionally paid)

**Time Limit**: **1 year** from date of payment of duty

**Procedure**:
- Application to Assistant/Deputy Commissioner
- With supporting documents
- Sanction order

---

### 6.2.11 Offences and Penalties

#### Smuggling (Section 135):

**Smuggling Defined**:
- Import/export contrary to provisions of Customs Act or other law
- Evasion of duty

**Penalty**:
- Goods liable to confiscation
- Fine up to 5 times value of goods OR ₹5,000 (whichever higher)
- Imprisonment: Up to **7 years** (if value > ₹1 crore: rigorous imprisonment)

---

#### Misdeclaration (Section 114):
- Undervaluation
- Misdescription
- Wrong classification

**Penalty**:
- Goods liable to confiscation
- Fine in lieu of confiscation
- Penalty equal to duty sought to be evaded

---

#### Non-Filing of Documents (Section 116):
- Failure to file bill of entry, shipping bill, manifest

**Penalty**: ₹50,000 or duty, whichever lower

---

#### Prohibited Goods (Section 111):
**Absolute confiscation**:
- No redemption on payment of fine
- Goods destroyed or disposed as per orders

**Examples**: Narcotic drugs, fake currency, obscene material, wildlife products

---

### 6.2.12 Appeals and Revision

#### Hierarchy:

**First Appeal** (Section 128):
- **To**: Commissioner (Appeals)
- **Within**: **60 days** from date of order
- **Condition**: Duty/penalty deposited (if demanded)

**Second Appeal** (Section 129A):
- **To**: Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal (CESTAT)
- **Within**: **3 months** from date of order

**Further Appeals**:
- **High Court**: On substantial question of law
- **Supreme Court**: Final appeal

### 6.2.14 Special Economic Zones (SEZ) (SEZ Act, 2005)

**SEZ**: Specially demarcated area for export-oriented units

**Benefits**:
1. **100% income tax exemption** (first 5 years)
2. **50% exemption** (next 5 years)
3. **Duty-free import** of capital goods, raw materials
4. **Exemption from GST** on supplies to SEZ
5. **No customs duty** on exports
6. **Single window clearance**

**Conditions**:
- Net foreign exchange earning positive
- Export obligations to be met
- Separate accounts maintained
