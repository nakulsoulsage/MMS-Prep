##### 5.1.3 Five Heads of Income
**Sources**: All three documents cover this extensively

###### A. Income from Salary (Sections 15-17)
- **Extracted Content**:
  - Section 15: Charging section, basis of charge
  - Section 16: Deductions from salary
  - Section 17: "Salary" definition
  - Employer-employee relationship
  - Rewards, wages, bonus, gratuity
  - Profits in lieu of salary
  - VRS (Voluntary Retirement Scheme)
  - TDS under Section 192
  - Provident Fund (EPFO) - 12% by employer + 12% by employee

- **Allowances under Section 10**:
  - Section 10(5): Leave Travel Allowance (LTA) - Travel within India
  - Section 10(13A): House Rent Allowance (HRA)
    - Formula: Minimum of [Actual HRA received, Rent paid - 10% of salary, 50% of salary for metro/40% for non-metro]
  - Section 10(14): Conveyance Allowance
  - Section 10(10): Gratuity (₹20 lakh limit)
    - Half month salary × completed years of service
    - Minimum 5 years of service required
  - Section 10(10AA): Leave Encashment (₹10 lakh limit, 10 months accumulation)
  - Section 10(10B): VRS compensation (₹5 lakh limit)
  - Traveling Allowance
  - WFH (Work From Home) Allowance
  - Research Allowance

- **Perquisites (Section 17)**:
  - Non-cash benefits from employer
  - Taxable perquisites vs exempt
  - Company loan at concessional rates

- **Standard Deduction**:
  - Amount: ₹50,000/₹75,000/₹1,00,000 (varies by year)

###### B. Income from House Property (Sections 22-27)
- **Extracted Content**:
  - Section 22: Charging section - ownership condition, annual value
  - Section 23: Annual value determination
    - Self-occupied property: 1 property exempt from tax
    - Let-out property: Actual rent or expected rent (whichever higher)
  - Section 24: Deductions allowed
    - Standard deduction: 30% of Net Annual Value (1/6th mentioned in Homi notes)
    - Interest on home loan: ₹2,00,000 limit per annum
  - Section 25-27: Other provisions

- **Co-ownership**:
  - Co-owners contribute to expenses in specific ratio
  - Income distributed in same ratio
  - Each co-owner files separate return for their share

- **Tenancy arrangements**:
  - Tenant property vs owner-occupied
  - Higher deductions for tenanted property
  - Tenant has exclusive right to stay

###### C. Income from Business or Profession (Sections 28-44)
- **Extracted Content**:
  - Section 28: Charging section
    - Profits/gains from business/profession taxable
    - Business defined under Section 2(13): adventure in nature of trade, commerce, manufacture
    - Commerce includes software development, trading
  - Section 29-37: Computation provisions
  - Section 32: Depreciation on assets
    - SLM (Straight Line Method) vs WDV (Written Down Value)
    - Rates: Residential building 5%, Machinery 15%, Commercial building 10%, Intangibles 25%
    - Assets ≤₹10,000: Zero depreciation (full expense)
  - Section 35AB: Technology/scientific research amortization (6 years)
  - Section 37: Business expenses (deductible if incurred wholly and exclusively)

- **Section 42A**: Conversion of stock-in-trade to capital assets

- **Presumptive Taxation Schemes**:
  - Section 44AD: Small businesses (₹2 crore turnover limit)
    - 8% of gross receipts (cash), 6% (digital)
  - Section 44ADA: Professionals (₹50 lakh limit)
    - 50% of gross receipts presumed as income
  - Section 44AE: Goods carriage business
  - Section 44B: Shipping business - 7.5% of gross receipts
  - Section 44BB: Oil exploration - 10%
  - Section 44BBA: Civil construction in turnkey projects - 5%
  - Section 44BBB: Manufacturing/production in mineral oil/gas - 10%

- **Leasing**:
  - Lessee, Lessor concepts
  - Front-end leasing, Back-end leasing
  - Balloon leasing

- **Business Income Considerations**:
  - Section 72A: Carry forward of losses in amalgamation/demerger
  - Section 115JB: MAT (Minimum Alternate Tax) on book profits
  - Unabsorbed depreciation: Can be carried forward indefinitely
  - Stock valuation methods

###### D. Capital Gains (Sections 45-55)
- **Extracted Content**:
  - Section 45: Charging section for capital gains
  - Section 2(14): Capital Assets definition
    - Exclusions: Stock-in-trade, personal effects (except jewelry), agricultural land in rural areas, gold deposit bonds, special bearer bonds, deposit certificates
  - Section 2(47): Transfer definition
    - Sale, exchange, relinquishment, extinguishment, compulsory acquisition

- **Types**:
  - STCG (Short Term Capital Gains): Holding period <36 months (12 months for equity shares)
    - Tax rate: 15% for equity shares
  - LTCG (Long Term Capital Gains): Holding period >36 months (12 months for equity shares)
    - Tax rate: 10% above ₹1 lakh exemption for equity

- **Carry forward**: Capital losses can be carried forward for 8 years
  - Can only be set off against capital gains (not other income)

- **Section 47**: Exemptions from capital gains
  - Gifts to relatives
  - Distribution of assets in HUF partition
  - Transfer under will/inheritance
  - Transfer to wholly owned subsidiary
  - Amalgamation/demerger transactions

- **ESOP (Employee Stock Option Plans)**:
  - Taxation on exercise of option
  - Taxation on sale of shares
  - 3-year lock-in period for tax benefits
  - STCG 15% if sold <36 months
  - LTCG 10% if sold >36 months (above ₹1 lakh)

###### E. Income from Other Sources (Sections 56-59)
- **Extracted Content**:
  - Section 56: Charging section
  - Dividends (mentioned in handwritten notes)
  - Interest income
  - Gifts above threshold
  - Income not covered under other four heads

- **Deductions**:
  - Section 80TTA: ₹10,000 interest on savings account (for individuals below 60 years)
  - Section 80TTB: ₹50,000 interest for senior citizens (60+ years)

#### 5.1.4 Gross Total Income (GTI)
- **Calculation**: Sum of income from all 5 heads after deductions under each head
- **Formula**: GTI = Salary + House Property + Business + Capital Gains + Other Sources

#### 5.1.5 Chapter VI-A Deductions (Sections 80C to 80U)
**Extracted Content from all sources**:

##### Major Deductions:
- **Section 80C**: ₹1,50,000 limit
  - LIC premium payments
  - PPF (Public Provident Fund)
  - EPF (Employer Provident Fund)
  - Home loan principal repayment
  - NSC (National Savings Certificate)
  - Equity Linked Saving Schemes (ELSS)
  - Tuition fees for children
  - Sukanya Samriddhi Account
  - SSY (Senior Citizens Saving Scheme)
  - 5-year bank FD

- **Section 80CCD(1B)**: NPS (National Pension Scheme) - Additional ₹50,000

- **Section 80D**: Medical insurance premium
  - Self/spouse/children: ₹25,000 (₹50,000 for senior citizens)
  - Parents: ₹25,000 (₹50,000 if senior citizens)
  - Maximum total: ₹1,00,000

- **Section 80E**: Education loan interest - No upper limit, 8 years

- **Section 80G**: Donations to charitable institutions
  - 50% or 100% deduction depending on institution
  - Some subject to 10% of GTI limit

- **Section 80TTA**: Savings account interest - ₹10,000

- **Section 80TTB**: Senior citizens bank interest - ₹50,000

- **Section 80U**: Handicapped persons deduction
  - ₹75,000 for persons with disability
  - ₹1,25,000 for persons with severe disability

#### 5.1.6 Total Income (Net Taxable Income)
- **Formula**: Total Income = GTI - Chapter VI-A deductions
- This is the final amount on which tax is calculated

#### 5.2 Tax Computation for Individuals

##### 5.2.1 Tax Regimes
**Content Sources**: LegalandTax_Notes.pdf, Homi Ranina Lecture 1

###### Old Tax Regime (Default)
**Tax Slabs**:
- Up to ₹2,50,000: NIL
- ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000: 5%
- ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000: 20%
- Above ₹10,00,000: 30%

**For Senior Citizens (60-80 years)**:
- Up to ₹3,00,000: NIL

**For Super Senior Citizens (80+ years)**:
- Up to ₹5,00,000: NIL

**Advantages**:
- All deductions under Chapter VI-A available
- Standard deduction of ₹50,000
- HRA, LTA exemptions available

###### New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC) - Optional
**Tax Slabs (as per latest)**:
- Up to ₹3,00,000: NIL
- ₹3,00,001 to ₹6,00,000: 5%
- ₹6,00,001 to ₹9,00,000: 10%
- ₹9,00,001 to ₹12,00,000: 15%
- ₹12,00,001 to ₹15,00,000: 20%
- Above ₹15,00,000: 30%

**Note**: Homi Lecture mentions:
- First 4 lakh (0-4 Lakhs): Exempted
- Next 4 Lakh (4-8 lakhs): 5%
- Next 4 Lakh (8-12 Lakhs): 10%
- Income 24 Lakh+: 30%
- 12 lakh taxable Income: NO tax (needs clarification - may refer to rebate)

**Disadvantages**:
- No deductions under Chapter VI-A (except 80CCD(2) employer NPS)
- No standard deduction
- No HRA, LTA exemptions

##### 5.2.2 Surcharge
**Progressive Rates**:
- ₹50 lakh to ₹1 crore: 10% surcharge
- ₹1 crore to ₹2 crore: 15% surcharge
- ₹2 crore to ₹5 crore: 25% surcharge
- Above ₹5 crore: 37% surcharge

##### 5.2.3 Health and Education Cess
- **Rate**: 4% on (Tax + Surcharge)
- Applicable on all taxpayers

##### 5.2.4 Rebate under Section 87A
- Available if total income ≤ ₹5,00,000
- Rebate: Lower of tax payable or ₹12,500
- Result: Effectively no tax up to ₹5 lakh (in old regime)

#### 5.3 Tax Payment Methods

##### 5.3.1 Advance Tax (Section 208)
**For Self-Employed and Business Income**:
- Applicable if tax liability > ₹10,000
- **Payment Schedule**:
  - By 15th June: 15% of total tax
  - By 15th September: 45% of total tax (30% additional)
  - By 15th December: 75% of total tax (30% additional)
  - By 15th March: 100% of total tax (25% additional)
- Based on estimated income for the financial year
- Interest under Section 234B and 234C for default

##### 5.3.2 TDS (Tax Deducted at Source)
**Section 192**: TDS on Salary
- Employer deducts tax before issuing salary
- Based on employee's income projections and tax declarations

**Other TDS Sections**:
- Section 194A: Interest
- Section 194C: Contractor payments
- Section 194H: Commission
- Section 194I: Rent
- Section 194J: Professional fees

##### 5.3.3 Self-Assessment Tax (Section 140A)
- Tax paid by taxpayer before filing ITR
- To avoid interest on shortfall
- Pays balance tax after TDS/Advance tax

#### 5.4 Concealed Income Provisions
**High-Tax Rate: 60% Flat Rate**

##### Section 68: Cash Credits
- Unexplained cash, loans, deposits in books
- Credibility of loan giver checked
- Burden of proof on taxpayer

##### Section 69: Unexplained Investments
- Investments not recorded in books or not explained
- Gold, shares, stocks, property
- GST receipts, income statements scrutinized

##### Section 69A: Unexplained Money, etc.
- Money, bullion, jewelry not recorded/explained
- Source of funds questioned

##### Section 69B: Amount Borrowed/Repaid on Hundi
- Unexplained borrowings or repayments

##### Section 69C: Unexplained Expenditure
- Expenses not recorded in books
- Marriage expenses, foreign/domestic travel
- Lifestyle expenses beyond declared income

##### Section 69D: Amount of Investments Not Fully Disclosed

**Section 115BBE**: Tax on Concealed Income
- **Tax Rate**: 60% (no deductions allowed)
- **Surcharge**: 25%
- **Cess**: 4%
- **Effective Rate**: ~78%

**Reopening of Cases**:
- Income Tax Department can reopen past returns up to 10 years
- Penalty: 200% of tax evaded (in some cases)

#### 5.5 Residential Status and Tax Liability
**Section 6: Residential Status Determination**

##### 5.5.1 Categories
**Extracted Content**: LegalandTax_Notes.pdf, Legal_Notes.pdf Page 7, Homi Ranina Lecture 2

###### A. Resident and Ordinarily Resident (ROR)
**Two Conditions (both must be met)**:
1. Physical presence in India for 182 days or more in current FY, OR
   - 60 days or more in current FY AND 365 days or more in preceding 4 FYs

2. Ordinarily resident test:
   - Resident in India in 2 out of 10 preceding years, AND
   - Physically present in India for 730 days or more in preceding 7 FYs

**Exception to 60-day rule (becomes 182 days)**:
- Indian citizen leaving India for employment abroad
- Indian citizen or Person of Indian Origin (PIO) coming to India on visit

**Tax Liability**:
- Global income taxable in India
- Income earned anywhere in world

###### B. Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR)
**Conditions**:
- Person who has been non-resident in 7 out of 9 preceding years
- Gets RNOR status for next 2 years

**Tax Liability**:
- Income earned in India: Taxable
- Income earned outside India: NOT taxable (for 2 years)
- Income from business controlled from India: Taxable

**FEMA Benefits**:
- Entitled to keep money outside India
- Foreign income not liable to tax in India for 2 years

###### C. Non-Resident (NR)
**Condition**:
- Does not satisfy conditions for ROR or RNOR
- Leave India before 27th September: Treated as NR for current FY

**Tax Liability**:
- Only India-sourced income taxable
- Foreign income: Not taxable

**Strategy**:
- Total days in India should not exceed 181 days aggregate
- No restriction on number of visits

#### 5.6 Foreign Exchange and Banking (FEMA)
**FEMA: Foreign Exchange Management Act**

##### 5.6.1 LRS (Liberalised Remittance Scheme)
- Every Resident Indian can remit up to USD 2,50,000 per FY
- Per individual limit
- Taxable if earning income outside and remitting

##### 5.6.2 Foreign Exchange Reserves
- India has USD 700 billion foreign exchange reserves
- RBI holds 660 tonnes of gold
- India has 25,000 tonnes of gold in possession

##### 5.6.3 Bank Account Types for NRIs

###### NRO Account (Non-Resident Ordinary)
- Savings account converted to NRO when moving abroad
- Rupee-denominated
- Interest: Taxable in India
- Repatriation: Limited

###### NRE Account (Non-Resident External)
- Foreign currency deposits, credited in Indian Rupees
- Higher interest rates (normal rupee deposit rate)
- Interest: **Exempt from tax** under Section 10(4)
- Freely repatriable
- Downside: Exchange rate risk (if rupee weakens, loss; if strengthens, gain)

###### FCNR Account (Foreign Currency Non-Resident)
- Deposits maintained in foreign currency
- Exchange rate at time of transaction
- Interest: **Exempt from tax** under Section 10(4)
- Fixed deposit account

###### RFC Account (Resident Foreign Currency)
- For returning NRIs
- Maintain foreign currency

###### EEFC Account (Exchange Earners Foreign Currency)
- For exporters and foreign exchange earners

#### 5.7 Clubbing of Income (Section 64)
**Concept**: Income of another person added to taxpayer's income

##### 5.7.1 Four Types of Clubbing

###### Type 1: Gift to Spouse
- Person makes gift to lawful spouse
- Income from gifted assets taxable in hands of donor
- Example: Husband gifts shares to wife, dividend income taxed to husband

###### Type 2: Minor Child's Income
- Income of minor child clubbed with parent having higher income
- Each child: ₹1,500 exemption per child
- **Exception**: Merit income (scholarships, prizes) not clubbed, taxed separately
- Guardian parent must file ITR

###### Type 3: Gift to Daughter-in-Law by Father-in-Law/Mother-in-Law
- Income from such gift clubbed with donor (FIL/MIL)
- Does NOT apply to gifts to Son-in-Law
- SIL pays tax on his own income from gifts

###### Type 4: Remuneration from Entity with Substantial Interest
- Person working in organization where spouse + relatives have ≥20% shareholding
- Salary clubbed with spouse's income
- **Relatives**: As defined in Section 2(41)
- **Exception**: If person has technical qualification and experience (commensurate salary), clubbing does not apply

##### 5.7.2 Gift to HUF
- If individual gifts to HUF, income from gifted assets clubbed with individual's income

#### 5.8 Special Tax Provisions

##### 5.8.1 Income from Gambling, Lottery, Online Games
- Tax Rate: 30% (highest rate)
- No deductions allowed
- Tax at source

##### 5.8.2 Principle of Taxation
**Diversion of Income vs Application of Income**:
- **Diversion of Income at Source**: Income diverted by overriding title before it reaches taxpayer
  - Result: NOT taxable to original earner
- **Application of Income**: Income first received, then applied/spent
  - Result: Taxable to earner

##### 5.8.3 Income of Mutuality
- Organizational income where contributors and participants are same
- Example: Co-operative housing societies (maintenance charges)
- Tax: EXEMPT
- Exception: Income from non-members (outsiders) is taxable

**Example**: BCCI cricket club
- Member contributions: Not taxable
- Services to non-members: Taxable

#### 5.9 Entities Liable to Pay Tax
**Content Source**: Homi Ranina Lecture 2

##### 5.9.1 Individuals
- Salary, capital gains, business income
- **Sole Proprietorship**: Individual is owner, business income taxed as individual
- Tax slabs as per individual rates

##### 5.9.2 HUF (Hindu Undivided Family)
- Hindu law concept
- Income from ancestral property
- Family business passed on through generations
- **Karta**: Head of HUF
  - Files 2 ITRs: One personal, one for HUF
- Separate PAN for HUF
- Tax slabs: Same as individual

##### 5.9.3 Partnership Firms
- Must be registered
- Tax Rate: **30% flat** on profits
- Partners: **NOT taxed again** on share from firm
- Partners taxed on salary/interest from firm

##### 5.9.4 LLP (Limited Liability Partnership)
- Tax Rate: **30% flat** on profits
- Partners: **NOT taxed again** on share from LLP

##### 5.9.5 Companies

###### Domestic Company
- **Tax Rates**:
  - New manufacturing companies: 15%
  - Companies under Section 115BA: 25%
  - Other companies: 30%
- Surcharge and cess additional
- Dividend Distribution Tax abolished (dividends taxed in hands of shareholders)

###### Foreign Company
- Tax Rate: **40%**
- Surcharge and cess additional

##### 5.9.6 AOP (Association of Persons)
- Private Trust
- Charitable Trust
- Religious Trust
- Public Trust

###### Private Trust
- Set up by rich individuals for next generations
- Tax must be paid before passing to beneficiaries
- Trustees file ITR

###### Charitable Trust
- Medical, education, poverty alleviation, heritage
- Tax Rate: Up to **30%**
- Must spend 85% of income on charitable activities
- Unspent amount: Taxable
- **Registration**: 2 certificates needed
  1. Charitable office registration
  2. Income tax department registration under Section 12A/12AA

**Example**: TATA Trusts
- Receives dividends from TATA Sons, TCS, other TATA companies
- Trusts for animals, education, healthcare
- Must spend 85% on beneficiaries

###### Religious Trust
- Religious donations liable for tax
- Registered political parties: **Exempt** from tax under Section 13A

##### 5.9.7 Co-operative Housing Society
- Formed under Society Act
- Members contribute maintenance charges
- Liable for tax on:
  - Interest income
  - Rental income from common areas
  - Non-member income
- Member contributions (maintenance): Exempt (principle of mutuality)

##### 5.9.8 Artificial Juridical Entity
- **Unique to India**
- **Deities** (Gods/Temples/Religious institutions)
- Person in charge of deity files ITR
- Taxed at individual rates

#### 5.10 Computation of Tax for Corporates

##### 5.10.1 Corporate Tax Structure
**Extracted Content**: LegalandTax_Notes.pdf

###### Domestic Companies
**Tax Rates**:
- Section 115BA (new manufacturing): 15%
- Section 115BAA: 22% (without exemptions)
- Section 115BAB: 15% (for new manufacturing companies)
- Regular rate: 25% (turnover < ₹400 crore)
- Regular rate: 30% (turnover ≥ ₹400 crore)

**Surcharge**:
- Income ₹1-10 crore: 7%
- Income > ₹10 crore: 12%

###### Foreign Companies
- Tax Rate: 40%
- Surcharge and cess additional

##### 5.10.2 MAT (Minimum Alternate Tax) - Section 115JB
- Applicable when book profit > tax profit
- Rate: 15% on book profits
- MAT credit can be carried forward for 15 years
- Set off against future tax liability

##### 5.10.3 Unabsorbed Depreciation
- Can be carried forward indefinitely
- No time limit
- Set off against future profits

##### 5.10.4 Section 72A: Amalgamation/Demerger
- Losses of amalgamating company can be carried forward by amalgamated company
- Conditions apply for carry forward
- Business continuity requirement

##### 5.10.5 DTAA (Double Tax Avoidance Agreement)
**Concepts**: LegalandTax_Notes.pdf

- Agreements with other countries to avoid double taxation
- **Indo-Mauritius DTAA**: Popular for investments
- **Permanent Establishment** (Article 5): Fixed place of business in another country
- Tax credit method or exemption method
- TRC (Tax Residency Certificate) required
