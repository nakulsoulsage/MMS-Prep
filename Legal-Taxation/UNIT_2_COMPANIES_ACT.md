## 2.1 INTRODUCTION TO COMPANIES ACT, 2013

### 2.1.1 Background and Overview

#### History:
- **Companies Act, 1956**: Earlier law governing companies (based on UK Companies Act, 1948)
- **Companies Act, 2013**: Replaced 1956 Act
- **Effective from**: April 1, 2014 (in phases)
- **Objective**: Corporate governance, transparency, accountability, investor protection

#### Key Changes from 1956 to 2013:
1. Introduction of **One Person Company (OPC)**
2. **Independent Directors** made mandatory
3. **Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR)** mandated
4. **Class Action Suits** allowed
5. **E-governance** provisions
6. **Women Directors** mandatory
7. **Audit Committee** for listed companies
8. Enhanced disclosure and compliance requirements

---

### 2.1.2 Definitions (Section 2)

#### Company (Section 2(20))
"A company incorporated under this Act or under any previous company law"

**Characteristics**:
- Artificial legal person
- Separate legal entity
- Perpetual succession
- Common seal
- Limited liability
- Transferability of shares
- Capacity to sue and be sued

---

#### Types of Companies:

##### 1. Based on Liability (Section 2(22), 2(21), 2(92)):

###### a) Company Limited by Shares (Section 2(22))
- Liability of members limited to unpaid amount on shares
- Most common type
- Can be public or private

###### b) Company Limited by Guarantee (Section 2(21))
- Liability of members limited to guaranteed amount
- No share capital (generally)
- Usually formed for charitable/non-profit purposes
- Example: Professional bodies, clubs, charities

###### c) Unlimited Company (Section 2(92))
- No limit on liability of members
- Rare in practice
- Members liable for entire debts

---

##### 2. Based on Access to Public (Sections 2(68), 2(71)):

###### a) Public Company (Section 2(71))
A company which:
- Is NOT a private company
- Has minimum 7 members (no maximum limit)
- Minimum paid-up capital: ₹5 lakh
- Can invite public to subscribe shares/debentures
- Must have at least 3 directors (7 for listed companies)
- Name ends with "Limited"

**Features**:
- Free transferability of shares
- No restriction on number of members
- Can raise funds from public
- Listed on stock exchange (if applicable)
- Greater regulatory compliance

###### b) Private Company (Section 2(68))
A company which by its Articles:
- **Restricts right to transfer shares**
- **Limits members to 200** (excluding employees/ex-employees)
- **Prohibits invitation to public** to subscribe shares/debentures
- Minimum 2 members, maximum 200
- Minimum paid-up capital: ₹1 lakh
- Minimum 2 directors
- Name ends with "Private Limited"

**Privileges**:
- Lesser compliance requirements
- No need to file prospectus
- Can start business immediately after incorporation
- Less stringent audit requirements
- More flexibility in management

---

##### 3. One Person Company (OPC) (Section 2(62))

**Definition**: Company with only ONE member

**Features**:
- Introduced in Companies Act, 2013
- Minimum 1 member, Maximum 1 member
- Minimum 1 director
- No minimum paid-up capital
- Limited liability
- Suitable for small entrepreneurs
- Name ends with "OPC Private Limited"

**Nominee**:
- Member must nominate a person
- Nominee becomes member in case of death/incapacity
- Nominee's consent required

**Restrictions**:
- Cannot carry on non-banking financial investment activities
- Cannot convert into Section 8 company
- Cannot be incorporated/converted into OPC if:
  - Paid-up capital exceeds ₹50 lakh, OR
  - Average annual turnover exceeds ₹2 crore

---

##### 4. Based on Control:

###### a) Holding Company (Section 2(46))
A company is holding company of another if:
- Controls composition of Board of Directors, OR
- Holds more than 50% nominal value of equity share capital, OR
- Subsidiary is subsidiary of subsidiary

###### b) Subsidiary Company (Section 2(87))
Company in which holding company:
- Controls composition of Board
- Holds more than 50% equity
- Is subsidiary of subsidiary

**Key Provisions**:
- Subsidiary cannot hold shares in holding company
- Separate accounts must be maintained
- Consolidated financial statements required

---

##### 5. Small Company (Section 2(85))

**Definition** (both conditions):
- Paid-up capital ≤ ₹4 crore (increased to ₹10 crore as per amendment)
- Turnover ≤ ₹40 crore (increased to ₹100 crore)

**Exclusions**: Not applicable to:
- Holding/subsidiary of non-small company
- Section 8 companies
- Companies registered under special Act

**Benefits**:
- Relaxed compliance requirements
- Less stringent financial statement requirements
- Rotation of auditors not required

---

##### 6. Section 8 Companies (Non-Profit) (Section 8)

**Objective**:
- Promotion of commerce, art, science, sports, education, research, social welfare, religion, charity, protection of environment

**Features**:
- No requirement of "Limited" in name
- Profits applied to promote objectives only
- No dividend to members
- License from Central Government required
- Can be company limited by shares or guarantee

## 2.2 INCORPORATION OF COMPANY

### 2.2.1 Pre-Incorporation Process

#### 1. Digital Signature Certificate (DSC)
- Promoters and Directors must obtain DSC
- For signing electronic documents

#### 2. Director Identification Number (DIN) (Section 153)
- **Mandatory** for every director
- Unique identification number
- Issued by Central Government (MCA)
- Application in **Form DIR-3**
- Lifetime validity

#### 3. Name Approval
- Application in **Form RUN (Reserve Unique Name)**
- Name should not be:
  - Identical or similar to existing company
  - Violating Emblems and Names Act
  - Suggesting government patronage
  - Undesirable
- Reserved for 20 days after approval

---

### 2.2.2 Incorporation Documents

#### 1. Memorandum of Association (MOA) (Section 4)
**"Constitution/Charter of Company"**

**Clauses**:
1. **Name Clause**: Name of company with "Limited/Private Limited/OPC"
2. **Registered Office Clause**: State where registered office situated
3. **Objects Clause**:
   - Main objects
   - Objects incidental to main objects
   - Other objects
4. **Liability Clause**: Limited or unlimited liability
5. **Capital Clause**:
   - Authorized capital
   - Division into shares of fixed amount
6. **Association/Subscription Clause**:
   - Signatures of subscribers
   - Minimum 7 for public, 2 for private, 1 for OPC
   - Witness required

**Alteration of MOA** (Sections 13, 61):
- Name: Special resolution + ROC approval
- Registered office: Special resolution + ROC filing
- Objects: Special resolution + notice to ROC
- Capital: As per Section 61

---

#### 2. Articles of Association (AOA) (Section 5)
**"By-laws/Internal rules of company"**

**Contents**:
- Rights and duties of members
- Powers and duties of directors
- Transfer of shares
- Meetings
- Dividends
- Winding up provisions

**Model Articles**: Table F (public), Table G, H, I (private)

**Alteration** (Section 14):
- Special resolution required
- Not inconsistent with Act or MOA
- Filed with ROC within 15 days

**MOA vs AOA**:

| MOA | AOA |
|-----|-----|
| Constitution of company | By-laws of company |
| Defines scope of activities | Defines internal management |
| Relationship with outsiders | Relationship between company and members |
| Mandatory for all companies | Optional (can adopt model articles) |
| Superior to AOA | Subordinate to MOA |
| Ultra vires acts void | Ultra vires acts can be ratified |

---

### 2.2.3 Incorporation Process

#### Documents Required (Section 7):
1. Memorandum of Association (MOA)
2. Articles of Association (AOA)
3. Form INC-7 (Application for incorporation)
4. Form INC-22 (Notice of situation of registered office)
5. Affidavit from directors (Form INC-9)
6. Proof of registered office address
7. PAN and TAN application
8. Specimen signature of directors
9. Payment of registration fees

#### Certificate of Incorporation (Section 7(2)):
- Issued by Registrar of Companies (ROC)
- Conclusive evidence of incorporation
- Company comes into existence from date of certificate
- Effects:
  - Company becomes legal entity
  - Perpetual succession
  - Can enter contracts
  - Can sue and be sued
  - Can own property

## 2.3 DIRECTORS

### 2.3.1 Definition and Types (Section 2(34))

**Director**: Member of Board of Directors

#### Types:

##### 1. Executive Directors
- Involved in day-to-day management
- Full-time employees
- Managing Director, Whole-time Director

##### 2. Non-Executive Directors
- Not involved in daily operations
- Part-time
- Attend board meetings

##### 3. Independent Directors (Section 149(6))
**Criteria**:
- Not managing/whole-time director
- Not promoter or related to promoter
- Not material business relationship with company
- Not relative of director/key personnel
- Not partner/executive of statutory auditor
- In opinion of Board, is independent

**Tenure**: Maximum 10 years (5+5 years)

**Duties**: Section 149(8) - Code for Independent Directors

**Number**:
- Listed companies: 1/3rd of total directors
- Other public companies: As per rules

##### 4. Woman Director (Section 149(1))
**Mandatory for**:
- Every listed company
- Public company with:
  - Paid-up capital ≥ ₹100 crore, OR
  - Turnover ≥ ₹300 crore

At least **ONE woman director**

##### 5. Nominee Director
- Appointed by financial institutions, government
- Represents lender's interests

##### 6. Alternate Director (Section 161(2))
- Appointed by Board
- Acts during absence of original director
- Same tenure as original director

##### 7. Additional Director (Section 161(1))
- Appointed by Board (not by shareholders)
- Holds office till next AGM

##### 8. Small Shareholders Director (Section 151)
- Listed companies may have
- Elected by small shareholders

---

### 2.3.2 Appointment of Directors (Sections 152, 160)

#### Number of Directors (Section 149):
- **Minimum**:
  - Public company: 3
  - Private company: 2
  - OPC: 1
- **Maximum**: 15 (can be increased by special resolution up to 15, beyond 15 needs Central Government approval)

#### Appointment:
1. **First Directors**: Named in AOA or by subscribers
2. **Subsequent Directors**: Elected by shareholders in General Meeting
3. **By Board**: Additional, alternate directors

#### Qualifications (Section 157):
- Must have DIN
- Not disqualified under Section 164
- Give consent to act as director
- Resident director: At least one director who stayed in India for 182 days in previous calendar year

#### Disqualifications (Section 164):
A person shall not be director if:
1. Of unsound mind
2. Undischarged insolvent
3. Applied for insolvency and application pending
4. Convicted of offense involving moral turpitude (5 years from conviction)
5. Not paid calls on shares for 6 months
6. Disqualified by court
7. Not filed financial statements or annual return for 3 years
8. Convicted under Act for 5 years

---

### 2.3.3 Powers and Duties of Directors

#### Powers of Board (Section 179):
1. Make calls on shares
2. Issue debentures
3. Borrow money
4. Invest funds
5. Make loans
6. Approve financial statements
7. Diversify business
8. Appoint managers
9. Other powers specified in AOA

**Restrictions** (Section 180):
Following require special resolution:
1. Sell/lease/dispose whole or substantially whole undertaking
2. Invest otherwise than in trust securities
3. Borrow money exceeding aggregate of paid-up capital, free reserves, securities premium
4. Remit/give time for payment of debt due by director

#### Duties of Directors (Section 166):

**Fiduciary Duties**:
1. Act in accordance with AOA
2. Act in good faith to promote objects
3. Act in best interest of company, members, employees, shareholders, community
4. Exercise due and reasonable care, skill, diligence
5. Not involve in situation of conflict of interest
6. Not achieve undue gain or advantage
7. Not assign office

**Additional Duties**:
- Duty to attend Board meetings
- Duty to disclose interest in contracts/arrangements
- Duty not to make secret profit
- Duty to disclose shareholding

---

### 2.3.4 Meetings of Board (Section 173)

#### Frequency:
- At least **4 meetings** in a year
- Maximum gap: **120 days** between two meetings
- At least **1 meeting** in each half of calendar year

#### Quorum (Section 174):
- 1/3rd of total strength or 2 directors, whichever higher
- Independent directors' meeting: 1/3rd of total independent directors or 2, whichever higher

#### Notice (Section 173(3)):
- At least **7 days** notice in writing
- Shorter notice allowed with consent of at least one independent director (if any)

#### Voting:
- Each director has one vote
- No proxy voting
- Chairperson has second/casting vote (if AOA permits)
- Resolution passed by majority
- Interested director cannot vote on contract in which he is interested

#### Minutes (Section 118):
- Maintained in book form or electronic
- Signed by chairperson of same meeting or next meeting
- Minutes are evidence of proceedings

---

## 2.4 KEY MANAGERIAL PERSONNEL (KMP) (Section 2(51))

### Definition:
- Chief Executive Officer (CEO)
- Managing Director (MD)
- Company Secretary (CS)
- Whole-time Director (WTD)
- Chief Financial Officer (CFO)
- Such other officer as prescribed

### Appointment (Section 203):

**Mandatory for**:
- Every listed company
- Every public company with paid-up capital ≥ ₹10 crore

**Requirements**:
- Must appoint: Managing Director/CEO/Manager, Company Secretary, CFO
- Full-time basis
- Can be one person for:
  - MD and CEO, OR
  - MD and Manager
- But MD/CEO/Manager AND CS/CFO must be different persons

---

### Managing Director/Whole-time Director (Sections 196-203):

#### Appointment:
- By Board resolution
- Approved by shareholders (ordinary resolution)
- Agreement stating terms
- Central Government approval (if remuneration exceeds limits)

#### Tenure:
- Maximum **5 years** at a time
- Can be reappointed
- Not less than one year notice for removal

#### Remuneration (Section 197):
**Limits**:
- One MD/WTD/Manager: **5%** of net profits
- More than one: **10%** of net profits
- No profits or inadequate profits: As per Schedule V

**Components**:
- Salary
- Commission
- Perquisites
- Sitting fees (not allowed)
- Stock options

**Approval**:
- Board approval
- Shareholders approval (ordinary resolution)
- If exceeds Schedule V limits: Special resolution + Central Government approval

---

### Company Secretary (Section 2(24), 205):

**Qualifications**:
- Member of Institute of Company Secretaries of India (ICSI)

**Role**:
- Compliance officer
- Conducts Board/General meetings
- Maintains statutory registers
- Files returns with ROC
- Liaison with regulatory authorities

**Key Responsibilities** (Section 205):
- Report to Board on compliance
- Ensure compliance with Act and other laws
- Other functions as prescribed

---

### Chief Financial Officer (CFO):

**Qualifications**:
- Chartered Accountant, Cost Accountant, or MBA Finance (as per company policy)

**Role**:
- Financial management
- Accounting
- Financial reporting
- Internal controls
- Certify financial statements with CEO

---

## 2.5 AUDIT AND AUDITORS

### 2.5.1 Appointment of Auditors (Sections 139-148)

#### Eligibility (Section 141):
**Can Be Auditor**:
- Chartered Accountant (individual or firm)
- In practice
- Certificate of Practice from ICAI

**Cannot Be Auditor** (Disqualifications):
1. Body corporate
2. Officer/employee of company
3. Partner/employee of director/KMP
4. Indebted to company > ₹5 lakh
5. Holding securities of company
6. Related to director/KMP
7. Convicted (6 months)
8. Providing prohibited services

---

#### Appointment Process (Section 139):

##### First Auditor:
- Appointed by Board within 30 days of incorporation
- If Board fails, members appoint within 90 days
- Holds office till conclusion of first AGM

##### Subsequent Auditors:
- Appointed by members in AGM
- Tenure: 5 years (for individuals) / 10 years (for firms)
- Rotation mandatory for listed companies
- Cannot be reappointed for 5 years after completion of tenure

**Rotation of Auditors** (Section 139(2)):
**For Listed Companies**:
- Individual auditor: 5 consecutive years
- Firm: 10 consecutive years (two terms of 5 years)
- Cooling-off period: 5 years

**Exemptions**:
- Private companies
- Government companies
- Companies with paid-up capital < ₹10 crore and turnover < ₹100 crore

---

#### Removal/Resignation (Section 140):

**Removal**:
- Special resolution by members
- Prior approval of Central Government required
- Auditor has right to be heard

**Resignation**:
- Auditor can resign by filing ADT-3
- Must state reasons
- Board must convene general meeting
- Cannot appoint same auditor for 5 years if resigned

---

### 2.5.2 Powers and Duties of Auditors (Section 143)

#### Rights of Auditors:
1. Access to books, accounts, vouchers
2. Obtain information and explanations
3. Visit branches and access branch books
4. Attend general meetings
5. Right to be heard
6. Receive notices of general meetings
7. Report to members

#### Duties of Auditors (Section 143):
1. **Auditor's Report** on:
   - True and fair view of financial statements
   - Compliance with accounting standards
   - Going concern status
   - Internal financial controls
   - Other matters as prescribed

2. **Report on Following**:
   - Whether proper books maintained
   - Whether balance sheet and profit & loss account comply with accounting standards
   - Whether financial statements give true and fair view
   - Whether any director is disqualified
   - Whether adequate internal financial controls exist
   - Any fraud by officers/employees reported

3. **Report to Central Government** (CARO):
   - If fraud detected
   - Report within 60 days
   - First to Audit Committee/Board
   - If no action, to Central Government

---

### 2.5.3 Audit Committee (Section 177)

**Mandatory for**:
- Every listed company
- Public companies with:
  - Paid-up capital ≥ ₹10 crore, OR
  - Turnover ≥ ₹100 crore, OR
  - Outstanding loans/borrowings/debentures ≥ ₹50 crore

**Composition**:
- Minimum **3 directors**
- **Majority**: Non-executive directors
- **Independent directors**: Majority
- **Chairperson**: Independent director

**Functions**:
1. Recommend appointment/remuneration/removal of auditor
2. Review financial statements before submission to Board
3. Approve related party transactions
4. Scrutinize inter-corporate loans
5. Valuation of undertakings/assets
6. Evaluate internal financial controls
7. Monitor vigil mechanism
8. Oversee financial reporting

**Meetings**:
- At least **4 meetings** per year
- Maximum gap: **120 days**

---

## 2.6 CORPORATE SOCIAL RESPONSIBILITY (CSR) (Section 135)

### Applicability:
Companies with (during immediately preceding financial year):
- Net worth ≥ ₹500 crore, OR
- Turnover ≥ ₹1,000 crore, OR
- Net profit ≥ ₹5 crore

---

### CSR Committee:
**Composition**: At least 3 directors (one independent director)

**Functions**:
1. Formulate CSR policy
2. Recommend amount to be spent
3. Monitor implementation

---

### CSR Expenditure:
**Obligation**: Spend at least **2% of average net profits** of preceding 3 years

**If Not Spent**:
- Transfer unspent amount to:
  - Schedule VII fund, OR
  - Fund specified for PM CARES or other approved fund
- Within 6 months of FY end

**Activities** (Schedule VII):
- Eradicating hunger, poverty, malnutrition
- Promoting education
- Promoting gender equality
- Reducing child mortality
- Maternal health
- Combating diseases (HIV/AIDS, etc.)
- Environmental sustainability
- Employment-enhancing vocational skills
- Social business projects
- Contribution to PM National Relief Fund
- Rural development projects
- Slum area development
- Disaster management

**Exclusions**:
- CSR activities for employees/families
- Activities outside India
- Sponsorships
- Political contributions

---

### Reporting:
- Board's Report must include:
  - CSR policy
  - Composition of CSR Committee
  - Amount to be spent
  - Amount actually spent
  - Reasons for shortfall (if any)

**Penalty** (Section 135(7)):
- Non-compliance: Fine ₹50,000 to ₹25 lakh
- Officer in default: Fine ₹50,000 to ₹5 lakh

---

## 2.7 RELATED PARTY TRANSACTIONS (Section 188)

### Related Party (Section 2(76)):

**Includes**:
1. Director or KMP
2. Relative of director/KMP
3. Firm in which director/KMP is partner
4. Private company of which director is director/member
5. Public company with 2% shareholding by director
6. Body corporate whose Board/managing director is accustomed to act on director's advice
7. Holding/subsidiary/associate company
8. Any person on whose advice Board accustomed to act

---

### Related Party Transactions:

**Transactions Requiring Approval** (Section 188):
1. Sale, purchase, supply of goods/materials
2. Selling or disposing of property
3. Leasing of property
4. Availing or rendering services
5. Appointment to any office/place of profit
6. Underwriting subscription of securities
7. Contracts for buying/selling goods > ₹1 crore or 10% of turnover, whichever lower

**Approval Required**:
- **Ordinary course of business at arm's length**: Board resolution
- **Not at arm's length OR Not in ordinary course**: Special resolution

**Audit Committee Approval**:
- Prior approval of Audit Committee required (for companies having Audit Committee)

---

### Disclosure:
- Director must disclose interest in contract/arrangement
- Interested director shall not participate in such Board meeting
- Details in Board's Report
- Details in financial statements (Note)

**Penalty**:
- Contract voidable at option of Board
- Non-compliant director liable to refund gain
- Company liable to penalty up to ₹25 lakh
- Officer liable to penalty up to ₹5 lakh

---

## 2.8 LOANS TO DIRECTORS (Section 185)

### Prohibition:
Company (except banking/insurance/NBFC) shall not give:
- Loan to director
- Loan to firm/company in which director is interested
- Guarantee/security for loan taken by director

---

### Exceptions:
1. Loan to MD/WTD as part of service conditions:
   - Approved by shareholders (special resolution)
   - Disclosed in financial statements

2. Loan to independent director: For working capital requirement

3. Company whose ordinary business includes lending:
   - At arm's length terms
   - In ordinary course

**Penalty**:
- Company: Punishment equivalent to amount involved
- Director: Imprisonment up to 6 months or fine up to ₹5 lakh or both

---

## 2.9 DIVIDEND (Sections 123-127)

### Declaration of Dividend (Section 123):

**Final Dividend**:
- Declared by shareholders in AGM
- On recommendation of Board
- Cannot exceed amount recommended by Board
- Paid out of:
  - Current year's profits (after providing for depreciation), OR
  - Previous years' profits (after providing for depreciation), OR
  - Both, OR
  - Government company from reserves subject to conditions

**Interim Dividend**:
- Declared by Board during the year
- Not require shareholders' approval

---

### Transfer to Reserves (Section 123(1)):
Before declaring dividend:
- No mandatory transfer to reserves (earlier 10% transfer was required)
- Company may voluntarily transfer

---

### Unpaid/Unclaimed Dividend (Section 124):
- Declared dividend not paid/claimed within **30 days**: Transfer to "Unpaid Dividend Account"
- After **7 years**: Transfer to "Investor Education and Protection Fund (IEPF)"
- Cannot be claimed back from IEPF (shares also transferred to IEPF)

---

### Restrictions on Payment (Section 123(4)):
Cannot pay dividend if:
- Call money on shares not paid
- From sale of assets (except insurance/banking company)

---

## 2.10 MEETINGS

### 2.10.1 Types of Meetings:

1. **Board Meetings** (Section 173): Already discussed
2. **General Meetings**:
   - Annual General Meeting (AGM) (Section 96)
   - Extraordinary General Meeting (EGM) (Section 100)
3. **Class Meetings** (Section 48): Meetings of particular class of shareholders

---

### 2.10.2 Annual General Meeting (AGM) (Section 96)

**Mandatory**: Every company must hold AGM

**Frequency**:
- Every calendar year
- Within **6 months** from end of financial year
- Gap between two AGMs: Not more than **15 months**

**First AGM**:
- Within **9 months** from close of first financial year
- Extension by 3 months allowed by ROC

**Venue**:
- Registered office or within same city/town/village
- National Capital Region (NCR) considered same place for Delhi companies

**Notice** (Section 101):
- At least **21 days** before meeting
- To every member, directors, auditors
- In writing (physical/electronic)
- Specify: Date, time, place, agenda
- For special business: Explanatory statement

**Business at AGM**:
**Ordinary Business**:
1. Consideration of financial statements and reports
2. Declaration of dividend
3. Appointment of directors in place of retiring directors
4. Appointment/reappointment of auditors

**Special Business**: Any business other than ordinary business

---

### 2.10.3 Extraordinary General Meeting (EGM) (Section 100)

**Purpose**: Any urgent business requiring shareholders' approval

**Convened by**:
1. **Board**: On its own or on requisition by:
   - Members holding ≥10% paid-up capital (with voting rights), OR
   - Members holding ≥10% voting power

2. **Tribunal**: If Board fails to convene within 45 days of requisition

3. **One or More Directors**: If no director/KMP available in India

**Notice**:
- At least **21 days** for special resolution
- At least **14 days** for other businesses
- Shorter notice allowed with consent of 95% members (paid-up capital with voting rights)

---

### 2.10.4 Resolutions (Section 114)

#### 1. Ordinary Resolution:
- Passed by **simple majority** (more than 50%)
- Default resolution type

#### 2. Special Resolution (Section 114(2)):
- Passed by **3/4th majority** (75%)
- Notice must specify resolution as special resolution

**Matters Requiring Special Resolution**:
- Alteration of MOA/AOA
- Change of name
- Change of registered office (outside local limits)
- Buy-back of shares
- Issue of sweat equity
- Reduction of capital
- Voluntary winding up
- Removal of auditor
- Related party transactions (not at arm's length)
- Loans to directors
- Many others as specified in Act

#### 3. Resolution by Circulation:
- Can be passed without meeting
- Sent to all directors
- 3/4th majority required
- Not valid for prescribed matters

---

### 2.10.5 Voting (Sections 107-109)

**Methods**:
1. **Show of Hands**: One vote per member (default)
2. **Poll**: Votes proportionate to shares held
   - Can be demanded by:
     - Members holding ≥10% voting power, OR
     - Members holding ≥₹5 lakh paid-up capital, OR
     - At discretion of chairman

**Postal Ballot** (Section 110):
- For specified businesses
- Through postal ballot or electronic voting
- Mandatory for certain resolutions

**Proxy** (Section 105):
- Member can appoint proxy
- Proxy need not be member
- Proxy cannot speak, can only vote on poll

**E-voting**:
- Listed companies must provide e-voting facility
- For all shareholder resolutions

---

### 2.10.6 Quorum (Section 103)

**General Meeting**:
- **5 members** personally present (for public company)
- **2 members** personally present (for private company)
- Unless AOA provides higher number

**If Quorum Not Present**:
- Adjourned to same day next week, same time, place
- If again no quorum: Members present constitute quorum

---

## 2.11 SHARE CAPITAL

### 2.11.1 Types of Capital:

1. **Authorized/Nominal Capital**: Maximum capital company can raise (in MOA)
2. **Issued Capital**: Part of authorized capital offered for subscription
3. **Subscribed Capital**: Part of issued capital actually subscribed
4. **Called-up Capital**: Part of subscribed capital called by company
5. **Paid-up Capital**: Part of called-up capital actually paid
6. **Reserve Capital**: Part not called but can be called in winding up only

---

### 2.11.2 Types of Share Capital (Section 43):

#### 1. Equity Share Capital:
- With voting rights
- Dividend not fixed, depends on profits
- Residual claim on assets
- Can be further classified:
  - With differential rights (DVR)
  - Sweat equity

#### 2. Preference Share Capital:
- Preferential rights as to:
  - Dividend (at fixed rate), AND
  - Repayment of capital in winding up
- Generally no voting rights (except on resolutions affecting their rights)

**Types of Preference Shares**:
- **Cumulative**: Unpaid dividend accumulates
- **Non-cumulative**: Unpaid dividend lapses
- **Redeemable**: Can be redeemed after certain period
- **Irredeemable/Perpetual**: Cannot be redeemed (now prohibited in India)
- **Participating**: Participate in surplus profits
- **Non-participating**: Fixed dividend only
- **Convertible**: Can be converted to equity
- **Non-convertible**: Cannot be converted

---

### 2.11.3 Issue of Shares

#### Public Issue:
- Through prospectus
- SEBI regulations apply
- Listed on stock exchange

#### Private Placement (Section 42):
- To select group (not more than 200 persons in FY)
- Through private placement offer letter
- Special resolution required
- Listed companies: Pricing as per SEBI regulations

#### Rights Issue (Section 62):
- To existing shareholders in proportion to existing shareholding
- Notice of at least 3 days but not more than 30 days
- If not subscribed, can be offered to others

#### Bonus Shares (Section 63):
- Issue of free shares to existing shareholders
- From free reserves/securities premium/capital redemption reserve
- Fully paid-up
- No payment required

#### Sweat Equity Shares (Section 54):
- Issued to employees/directors
- At discount or for consideration other than cash
- For providing know-how or intellectual property rights
- Special resolution required

---

### 2.11.4 Increase and Reduction of Capital

#### Increase of Capital (Section 61):
- Authorized capital: Alter MOA by ordinary resolution + fee to ROC
- Issued capital: Issue new shares as per provisions

#### Reduction of Capital (Section 66):
- Special resolution
- Tribunal approval
- Notice to creditors
- Methods:
  - Extinguish liability on unpaid shares
  - Cancel paid-up capital lost/unrepresented by assets
  - Pay off excess capital

---

## 2.12 COMPROMISES, ARRANGEMENTS, AMALGAMATIONS (Sections 230-240)

### 2.12.1 Schemes of Arrangement:

**Types**:
1. **Compromise/Arrangement**: Between company and creditors/members
2. **Amalgamation**: Merger of two or more companies
3. **Demerger**: Division of company

---

### 2.12.2 Procedure (Section 230-232):

1. **Application to Tribunal (NCLT)**
2. **Tribunal orders meeting** of creditors/members
3. **Notice of meeting** (21 days)
4. **Meeting held**, scheme explained
5. **Voting**:
   - Majority in number (more than 50%), AND
   - 3/4th in value
6. **Tribunal approval**:
   - After approval by members/creditors
   - Tribunal examines fairness
   - Hearing of objections
7. **Order sanctioning scheme**
8. **Filing with ROC**
9. **Scheme becomes effective**

---

### 2.12.3 Effects of Amalgamation:

- Transferor company dissolved without winding up
- All assets and liabilities transfer to transferee company
- Shareholders of transferor become shareholders of transferee
- Contracts, agreements continue
- Legal proceedings continue

---

## 2.13 WINDING UP (Sections 270-365)

### 2.13.1 Meaning:
Process of bringing company's life to an end

### 2.13.2 Modes of Winding Up:

#### 1. Voluntary Winding Up (Sections 304-323):
**Types**:
a) **Members' Voluntary Winding Up**: Company is solvent
b) **Creditors' Voluntary Winding Up**: Company is insolvent

**Grounds**:
- Period fixed by AOA expired
- Event occurred on which company to be dissolved
- Special resolution for voluntary winding up
- Company unable to pay debts
- Tribunal orders winding up

#### 2. Winding Up by Tribunal (Sections 271-303):

**Grounds (Section 271)**:
1. Special resolution for winding up by Tribunal
2. Company defaults in filing financial statements/annual return
3. Tribunal satisfied company unable to pay debts
4. Acted against sovereignty, integrity, security of India
5. Tribunal opinion just and equitable to wind up
6. Company made default in repayment of deposits
7. Company unable to pay debts

---

### 2.13.3 Procedure:

**Voluntary Winding Up**:
1. Board meeting approving voluntary winding up
2. General meeting passing special resolution
3. Appointment of liquidator
4. Notice to ROC and Official Liquidator
5. Publication in Official Gazette and newspaper
6. Liquidator takes over assets
7. Settlement of debts
8. Distribution to members
9. Final meeting and dissolution

**Compulsory Winding Up (by Tribunal)**:
1. Petition to Tribunal
2. Tribunal's order to wind up
3. Appointment of Official Liquidator
4. Taking control of assets
5. Settlement with creditors
6. Distribution of surplus (if any)
7. Dissolution order

---

### 2.13.4 Order of Payment in Winding Up (Section 327):

**Priority**:
1. **Secured creditors**: From secured assets
2. **Workmen's dues**: Wages/salaries (4 months before winding up, up to ₹20,000 per workman)
3. **Debts due to government**: Taxes, duties
4. **Unsecured creditors**: Proportionately
5. **Preference shareholders**: Return of capital
6. **Equity shareholders**: Surplus distributed proportionately

---

## 2.14 DEPOSITS (Sections 73-76)

### 2.14.1 Acceptance of Deposits:

**Who Can Accept**:
- Public company (subject to rules)
- Private company from members (no limit)

**Who Cannot Accept**:
- Banking companies
- NBFCs
- Companies as prescribed

**Limits**:
- Maximum 25% of aggregate of paid-up capital and free reserves
- Can be increased to 35% with credit rating

---

### 2.14.2 Conditions:
1. Issue circular to shareholders
2. File circular with ROC
3. Credit rating (if accepting from public)
4. Create deposit repayment reserve
5. Provide security (if accepting from public)
6. Repayment on time

**Period**:
- Minimum 6 months
- Maximum 36 months

**Renewal**: Cannot be renewed beyond 36 months from date of first deposit

---

### 2.14.3 Default:
**Penalty**:
- Company: Fine ₹1 crore to ₹10 crore
- Officer: Imprisonment up to 7 years + fine ₹25 lakh to ₹2 crore

**Tribunal Power**:
- Order repayment with interest

---

## 2.15 CHARGES (Sections 77-87)

### 2.15.1 Meaning:
Security interest created on company's assets in favor of lender

### 2.15.2 Types:
1. **Fixed Charge**: On specific assets (land, building, machinery)
2. **Floating Charge**: On current assets (stock, debtors, cash)

---

### 2.15.3 Registration of Charges (Section 77):

**Mandatory Registration with ROC**:
- Within **30 days** of creation
- Form CHG-1
- Late filing: Up to 300 days with additional fee

**Particulars to Be Registered**:
- Date of creation
- Amount secured
- Property charged
- Name of charge holder
- Terms of charge

**Non-Registration Effect**:
- Charge void against liquidator and creditors
- Debt becomes immediately payable

---

### 2.15.4 Modification/Satisfaction of Charge:
- File Form CHG-4 (satisfaction) within 30 days
- File Form CHG-9 (modification) within 30 days
- ROC enters particulars in register

---

## 2.16 PREVENTION OF OPPRESSION AND MISMANAGEMENT (Sections 241-246)

### 2.16.1 Application to Tribunal:

**By Whom** (Section 244):
- Members holding ≥10% of issued share capital or ≥10% of members (whichever less)
- In case of company not having share capital: ≥20% of members or ≥100 members (whichever less)
- Central Government

**Grounds** (Section 241):

#### Oppression:
- Affairs conducted in manner oppressive to member(s)
- Disregard of interests of member(s)
- Material change in management/control prejudicial to interests

#### Mismanagement:
- Material change in management/control likely to affect prejudicially:
  - Company's interests
  - Public interest

**Examples**:
- Diversion of funds
- Refusal to register transfer of shares
- Denial of inspection of books
- Non-payment of dividend despite profits
- Allotment of shares to director's relatives at undervalue
- Suppression of material information

---

### 2.16.2 Powers of Tribunal (Section 242):

May pass orders:
1. Regulation of conduct of company's affairs
2. Purchase of shares of members by other members/company
3. Reduction of capital
4. Termination/setting aside of agreements
5. Removal of managing director/director
6. Recovery of undue gains
7. Setting aside of transactions
8. Appointment of new directors
9. Any other order as deemed fit

---

## 2.17 SIGNIFICANT BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP (Sections 89-90)

### Purpose:
- Identify ultimate beneficial owners
- Prevent money laundering
- Transparency in ownership

### Declaration (Section 89):
- Individual holding shares beneficially must declare
- Company must maintain register of significant beneficial owners
- Threshold: Holding ≥10% shares or voting rights (directly or indirectly)

### Filing:
- Form BEN-1 (by individual)
- Form BEN-2 (by company)

## 2.18 INSIDER TRADING AND PROHIBITION

### Insider Trading:
- Trading in securities while in possession of unpaid price sensitive information (UPSI)
- Prohibited under SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015

### Insider:
- Director, KMP, employee
- Connected person
- Person having access to UPSI

### Prohibition:
- Trading during closure of trading window
- Communicating UPSI to others
- Procuring securities for others using UPSI

**Penalty**:
- Imprisonment up to 10 years or fine up to ₹25 crore or both

---

## 2.19 PENALTIES AND ADJUDICATION

### Penalties Framework:
- Based on severity of offense
- Monetary penalties and/or imprisonment
- Compounding of offenses allowed (Section 441)

### Adjudication:
- Regional Director has power to adjudicate
- Special Courts for trial of offenses
- National Financial Reporting Authority (NFRA) for accounting/auditing standards violations
