## 3.1 CONSUMER PROTECTION ACT, 2019

### 3.1.1 Introduction

#### Background:
- **Consumer Protection Act, 1986**: Original legislation
- **Consumer Protection Act, 2019**: Replaced 1986 Act
- **Effective from**: July 20, 2020
- **Objective**: Protect rights of consumers, provide speedy and simple redressal

#### Why Consumer Protection?
- Information asymmetry between sellers and buyers
- Weak bargaining power of consumers
- Exploitation by unfair trade practices
- Defective goods and deficient services
- Need for speedy and inexpensive remedy

---

### 3.1.2 Definitions (Section 2)

#### Consumer (Section 2(7))
"Any person who:
- **Buys goods** for consideration (paid, promised, partly paid), AND
- **Uses such goods** (with approval of buyer)
- **Hires/avails services** for consideration (paid, promised, partly paid, under any system)

**BUT does NOT include**:
- Person who obtains goods for **resale**
- Person who obtains goods for **commercial purpose**"

**Commercial Purpose Exceptions** (Included as consumers):
- Goods/services used for earning livelihood by:
  - Self-employment, OR
  - Utilized for commercial purpose (≤₹1 crore)

**Examples**:
- ✅ **Consumer**: Person buying car for personal use
- ✅ **Consumer**: Person buying laptop for home use
- ✅ **Consumer**: Small shopkeeper buying goods for resale (≤₹1 crore)
- ❌ **Not Consumer**: Wholesaler buying for resale
- ❌ **Not Consumer**: Company buying raw materials for manufacturing

---

#### Goods (Section 2(15))
- Every kind of movable property
- Includes food, free or concessional goods provided under statutory/welfare scheme

---

#### Services (Section 2(42))
"Service of any description made available to potential users"

**Includes**:
- Banking, financing, insurance, transport
- Processing, supply of electrical/other energy
- Board/lodging, housing construction
- Entertainment, amusement, purveying news
- Professional services (but NOT free services or under contract of personal service)

**Exclusions**:
- Services rendered free of charge
- Under contract of personal service (master-servant)

---

#### Defect (Section 2(10))
"Any fault, imperfection, shortcoming in:
- **Quality**, **quantity**, **potency**, **purity**, **standard**
- As required by law or contract (express/implied)
- As claimed by trader"

---

#### Deficiency (Section 2(11))
"Any fault, imperfection, shortcoming, inadequacy in:
- **Quality**, **nature**, **manner** of performance
- As required by law or contract (express/implied)
- As claimed by service provider"

**Includes**:
- Act of omission, commission, negligence
- Withholding of services
- Not maintaining standard in relation to services

---

#### Unfair Trade Practice (Section 2(47))
"A trade practice which for promoting sale/supply/services adopts any **unfair method or deceptive practice**"

**Includes**:
1. **False Representation**:
   - Standard, quality, grade, composition, style, model
   - Place of origin, manufacturer, approval
   - Sponsorship, affiliation
   - Existence of performance characteristics, benefits, uses
   - Warranty, guarantee, undertaking
   - Price discounts

2. **False Offer**:
   - Bargain price when actual intention is to sell higher price
   - Gifts, prizes, other items free of cost when conditions attached

3. **Non-Compliance**:
   - With prescribed standards
   - With weights and measures laws

4. **Misleading Advertisement**:
   - Publication, announcement, notification
   - False or misleading facts

5. **Hoarding/Destruction**:
   - Creating artificial scarcity
   - Raising prices

---

#### Misleading Advertisement (Section 2(28))
"Advertisement which:
- Falsely describes product/service, OR
- Gives false guarantee, OR
- Material information concealed, OR
- Unfair trade practice adopted, OR
- Not in conformity with prescribed manner"

---

#### Product Liability (Section 2(34))
"Responsibility of:
- Product manufacturer
- Product service provider
- Product seller

To compensate for harm caused by defective product or deficiency in services"

---

### 3.1.3 Rights of Consumers (Section 2(9))

#### 6 Consumer Rights:

##### 1. Right to Safety (Protection from hazardous goods/services)
- Protection against marketing of goods/services hazardous to life/property
- Examples:
  - Unsafe electrical appliances
  - Adulterated food
  - Harmful drugs
  - Defective vehicles

##### 2. Right to be Informed
- Information about quality, quantity, potency, purity, standard, price
- Protection against unfair trade practices
- Right to know before purchase

##### 3. Right to Choose
- Access to variety of goods/services at competitive prices
- Right to be assured of satisfactory quality and service
- Freedom of choice

##### 4. Right to be Heard
- Right to represent interests in appropriate forums
- Right to voice complaints
- Right to fair hearing

##### 5. Right to Seek Redressal
- Right to seek compensation
- Right to fair settlement of genuine grievances
- Redressal forums at district, state, national levels

##### 6. Right to Consumer Education
- Right to acquire knowledge and skills
- To be informed consumer
- Aware of rights and responsibilities

---

### 3.1.4 Responsibilities of Consumers

#### Consumer Duties:
1. **Be Aware**: Rights and remedies available
2. **Be Cautious**: Think before buying, check quality and price
3. **Be Assertive**: Exercise rights, complain for defects
4. **Be Organized**: Form consumer groups
5. **Be Quality Conscious**: Purchase standardized products (ISI, Agmark, Hallmark)
6. **Be Honest**: Do not engage in black-marketing, hoarding

---

### 3.1.5 Consumer Protection Councils

#### Central Consumer Protection Council (CCPC) (Section 4):
**Constitution**: Central Government
**Chairperson**: Union Minister (Consumer Affairs)
**Members**:
- Other official and non-official members representing:
  - Consumers, manufacturers, traders
- Not exceeding 100 members

**Objectives**:
- Promote and protect consumer rights
- Advise Central Government on consumer policy
- Review consumer protection laws

---

#### State Consumer Protection Council (SCPC) (Section 7):
**Constitution**: State Government
**Chairperson**: Minister in-charge (Consumer Affairs)
**Members**: Similar to CCPC

---

#### District Consumer Protection Council (DCPC) (Section 8):
**Constitution**: District administration
**Chairperson**: District Collector

---

### 3.1.6 Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) (Section 10)

#### Establishment:
- Central Government shall establish CCPA
- To regulate matters related to consumer rights violations
- To prevent unfair trade practices and misleading advertisements

#### Composition (Section 11):
- **Chief Commissioner**
- **2 Commissioners** (appointed by Central Government)
- Other officers and employees

**Qualifications**:
- Chief Commissioner: High Court Judge or District Judge (at least 5 years), or have special knowledge/experience ≥25 years in consumer affairs/trade/commerce/law
- Commissioners: Experience ≥15 years

**Tenure**: 5 years or 65 years (whichever earlier)

---

#### Powers and Functions (Sections 18-19):

**Powers**:
1. **Inquire/investigate** consumer rights violations
2. **Recall products** (if hazardous)
3. **Order refund** or replacement
4. **Direct discontinuance** of unfair trade practices
5. **Pass interim orders**
6. **Impose penalties**
7. **File class action suits**
8. **Issue safety notices**

**Specific Powers**:
- Powers of civil court (Section 20):
  - Summon witnesses
  - Require production of documents
  - Receive evidence on affidavits
  - Requisition public records
  - Issue commissions

---

#### Penalties (Sections 21, 89):

##### Misleading Advertisement:
**Manufacturer/Endorser**:
- First time: **₹10 lakh** fine
- Second time onwards: **₹50 lakh** fine
- Endorser: Prohibition from making endorsement up to **1 year** (first), **3 years** (subsequent)

##### Unfair Trade Practice:
- Manufacturer/Trader: **₹25 lakh** fine (first), **₹50 lakh** (subsequent)

##### Non-Compliance with CCPA Orders:
- Imprisonment up to **3 years** + fine up to **₹1 lakh**

---

### 3.1.7 Consumer Dispute Redressal Mechanism

#### Three-Tier System:

##### 1. District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (District Commission) (Section 34)

**Jurisdiction** (Section 34(2)):
- **Pecuniary**: Goods/services value ≤ **₹1 crore**
- **Territorial**: Within district limits

**Composition** (Section 27):
- **President**: District Judge or qualified to be District Judge
- **Members**: 2-4 members (at least one woman)
  - Experience in law, economics, commerce, accountancy, industry, public affairs, administration

**Appointment**: State Government

---

##### 2. State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (State Commission) (Section 42)

**Jurisdiction** (Section 42(2)):
- **Pecuniary**: Goods/services value > **₹1 crore** but ≤ **₹10 crore**
- **Territorial**: Within state
- **Appellate**: Appeals from District Commission orders

**Composition** (Section 42):
- **President**: High Court Judge or qualified to be High Court Judge
- **Members**: 4-14 members (at least one woman)

**Appointment**: State Government

---

##### 3. National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (National Commission) (Section 50)

**Jurisdiction** (Section 58(1)):
- **Pecuniary**: Goods/services value > **₹10 crore**
- **Pan-India Territorial Jurisdiction**
- **Appellate**: Appeals from State Commission orders

**Composition** (Section 50):
- **President**: Supreme Court Judge or qualified to be Supreme Court Judge
- **Members**: 4-11 members (at least one woman)

**Appointment**: Central Government

---

### 3.1.8 Complaint and Redressal Procedure

#### Who Can File Complaint? (Section 35)

1. **Consumer**
2. **Voluntary Consumer Association** (registered)
3. **Central/State Government**
4. **One or more consumers** (where interests are same)
5. **Legal heir** or representative of deceased consumer
6. **CCPA** (Central Consumer Protection Authority)

---

#### Against Whom Complaint Can Be Filed? (Section 2(7), 2(42))

1. **Manufacturer** or **Producer**
2. **Trader** or **Seller**
3. **Service Provider**
4. **Product Liability** cases: Manufacturer, seller, service provider

---

#### Grounds for Complaint (Section 35(1)):

1. **Unfair/Restrictive Trade Practice** adopted
2. **Goods sold are defective**
3. **Services hired are deficient**
4. **Price charged is excess** of price:
   - Fixed by law, OR
   - Displayed on goods, OR
   - Agreed between parties
5. **Goods/services hazardous** to life/safety
6. **Offer of spurious goods** for sale
7. **Misleading advertisement** regarding goods/services

---

#### Time Limit for Filing Complaint (Section 69):
- **Within 2 years** from date when:
  - Cause of action arose, OR
  - Defect discovered
- **Condonation**: Can be extended if sufficient cause shown

---

#### Contents of Complaint (Section 35(1)):

Must contain:
1. Name, description, address of complainant
2. Name, address of opposite party
3. Facts relating to complaint
4. Documents supporting allegations
5. Relief sought

---

#### Complaint Filing:

**Mode**:
- Written form or **electronic form**
- Affidavit supporting complaint
- Fee (if applicable)

**Fee Structure** (Section 58):
- Up to ₹5 lakh: ₹200
- ₹5 lakh to ₹10 lakh: ₹400
- ₹10 lakh to ₹20 lakh: ₹500
- Above ₹20 lakh: ₹5,000

---

### 3.1.9 Procedure before Commission

#### Admission (Section 36):
- Commission examines complaint
- If prima facie case: Admits
- If no prima facie case: Rejects (with reasons)
- If incomplete: Opportunity to rectify

---

#### Notice to Opposite Party (Section 38):
- Commission issues notice
- Opposite party must file reply within **30 days**
- Can be extended (for sufficient cause)

---

#### Hearing:
- Both parties heard
- Evidence recorded
- Documents examined
- Commission can:
  - Call for additional information
  - Require sample/testing
  - Appoint expert

---

#### Settlement (Section 37):
- Commission may refer to **mediation** if:
  - Both parties agree
  - Case suitable for mediation
- **Mediation Cell** attached to each Commission
- If settlement reached: Recorded and enforceable as order
- If no settlement: Complaint proceeds to adjudication

---

#### Powers of Commission (Section 37):

##### Investigation Powers:
1. Require production of documents
2. Issue summons to witnesses
3. Requisition goods for testing
4. Order inspection
5. Powers of civil court

##### Testing of Goods:
- Can send sample to appropriate lab
- Opposite party bears cost (if found defective)
- Test report is evidence

---

### 3.1.10 Orders/Reliefs (Section 37)

#### Types of Orders Commission Can Pass:

1. **Remove defect** in goods
2. **Replace** defective goods with new goods
3. **Return price** paid
4. **Pay compensation** for:
   - Loss or injury suffered
   - Mental agony and harassment
   - Loss of time, costs
5. **Discontinue** unfair/restrictive trade practice
6. **Not to offer** hazardous goods for sale
7. **Withdraw** hazardous goods from sale
8. **Cease** misleading advertisement
9. **Corrective advertisement** to neutralize misleading ad
10. **Provide adequate cost** for complaint
11. **Punitive damages** (in appropriate cases)
12. **Any other relief** as deemed fit

---

#### Compensation:

**Components**:
- **Actual Loss**: Cost of goods/services
- **Consequential Loss**: Loss flowing from defect
- **Mental Agony**: Harassment, inconvenience (generally ₹10,000 to ₹2,00,000)
- **Litigation Costs**: Expenses incurred in filing complaint
- **Interest**: From date of filing till payment

---

### 3.1.11 Product Liability (Sections 82-87)

#### Product Liability Action (Section 82):
Claim for compensation against:
- **Product manufacturer**
- **Product service provider**
- **Product seller**

For harm caused by defective product or deficiency in services

---

#### Who Can File? (Section 83)

- Any person affected
- No need to be purchaser
- Can be user, consumer, or any person harmed

---

#### Grounds (Section 84):

**Against Manufacturer/Service Provider**:
1. **Manufacturing defect**
2. **Design defect**
3. **Deviation from manufacturing specifications**
4. **Inadequate/incorrect information** on use/side effects/dangers
5. **Failure to exercise care**

**Against Seller**:
1. Seller has **assembled/manufactured** product
2. Seller has not **correctly maintained/kept product**
3. **Improper storage**
4. Seller cannot **identify manufacturer** within 30 days

---

#### Defenses Available (Section 85):

Manufacturer/seller/service provider NOT liable if proves:
1. Product not put in stream of commerce by him
2. Defect did not exist when product was delivered
3. Product not manufactured for sale/commercial purpose
4. Defect is due to compliance with law
5. No scientific/technical knowledge of defect at time of supply
6. Manufacturer of component not liable if defect was in design of product itself
7. Consumer knew defect
8. Consumer altered/misused product

---

### 3.1.12 Appeal (Sections 51, 66, 71)

#### Appeal from District Commission:
- **To**: State Commission
- **Within**: 30 days (45 days for state/district forum, Central Government)
- **Extension**: Can be extended up to 45 days if sufficient cause

#### Appeal from State Commission:
- **To**: National Commission
- **Within**: 30 days (45 days for state/district forum, Central/State Government)

#### Appeal from National Commission:
- **To**: Supreme Court of India
- **Within**: 30 days
- **On**: Question of law only

---

### 3.1.13 Execution of Orders (Section 72)

#### Enforcement:
- Orders of Commission are **binding**
- Enforceable as **decree of civil court**
- Can be executed through:
  - District forum: As First Class Magistrate's order
  - State Commission: As High Court decree
  - National Commission: As Supreme Court decree

#### Non-Compliance Penalty:
- Imprisonment: **Minimum 1 month, Maximum 3 years**
- Fine: Minimum **₹25,000**

---

### 3.1.14 Limitation and Bar

#### Limitation Period (Section 69):
- **2 years** from date when cause of action arose
- Condonation possible with sufficient cause

#### Conclusiveness (Section 52):
- Orders of Commission conclusive
- Cannot be questioned in any court
- Except through appeal to higher commission or Supreme Court

---

### 3.1.15 E-Commerce Transactions (Section 2(16))

#### E-Commerce Defined:
"Buying or selling of goods or services including digital products over digital or electronic network"

#### Applicability:
- Consumer Protection Act fully applicable to e-commerce
- E-commerce entities covered as service providers
- Marketplace and inventory models both covered

**Obligations of E-Commerce Entities** (Rules):
1. Provide information about:
   - Return, refund, exchange, warranty, delivery, payment terms
   - Country of origin
   - Seller details
2. Not to impose cancellation charges on consumers cancelling after confirmation of purchase
3. Acknowledge receipt of pre-paid order within 5 days
4. Effect delivery within 30 days or inform consumer
5. Not mislead consumers

**Liability**:
- For defective goods/deficient services
- For misleading advertisement
- For non-delivery/delayed delivery

---

### 3.1.16 Direct Selling (Section 2(13))

#### Direct Selling Defined:
"Marketing, distribution, sale of goods/services through person-to-person (not fixed retail location) to consumers"

**Includes**: Network marketing, multi-level marketing

#### Direct Selling Entity Obligations:
1. Not to promote pyramid scheme or money circulation scheme
2. Provide information about products, buyback policy
3. Ensure direct sellers are trained
4. Not to induce consumers to make payment for recruitment
5. Allow return within 30 days

**Prohibition**:
- Pyramid schemes prohibited
- Money circulation schemes prohibited

---

### 3.1.17 Mediation (Section 74)

#### Mediation Cell (Section 74):
- Attached to each Commission
- For settlement of consumer disputes

#### Procedure:
1. Parties consent for mediation
2. Referred to mediation cell
3. Mediator facilitates settlement
4. Settlement recorded
5. Signed by parties and mediator
6. Forwarded to Commission
7. Commission passes order as per settlement
8. Order is final and binding

**Time Limit**: Within **prescribed time** (generally 90 days)

**If Settlement Fails**:
- Matter returned to Commission for adjudication

---

### 3.1.18 Class Action (Section 36)

#### Who Can File:
- Voluntary consumer association
- Central/State Government
- 15 or more consumers having same interest

#### Grounds:
- Same trade practice affects numerous persons
- Common defect in goods/deficiency in service
- Filing individual complaints not practical

#### Relief:
- Compensation to all affected consumers
- Refund of price
- Recall of goods

---

### 3.1.19 Key Provisions for Business/Management

#### Importance for Managers:

##### 1. Compliance Requirements:
- Ensure products meet quality standards
- Provide accurate information
- Avoid misleading advertisements
- Maintain proper records of complaints

##### 2. Risk Management:
- Product liability insurance
- Quality control systems
- Customer grievance redressal mechanism
- Training of customer-facing staff

##### 3. Advertisement Guidelines:
- Truthful and accurate claims
- Substantiate claims with evidence
- Avoid exaggeration
- Celebrity endorsers can be held liable

##### 4. Warranty and Service:
- Clear warranty terms
- Efficient service network
- Timely redressal of complaints
- Maintain service records

##### 5. E-Commerce:
- Transparent pricing
- Clear return/refund policy
- Accurate product description
- Secure payment systems
- Timely delivery

##### 6. Documentation:
- Maintain records of:
  - Product testing reports
  - Customer complaints and actions taken
  - Warranties given
  - Service provided
  - Advertisement approvals

### 3.1.20 Landmark Consumer Cases

#### 1. Indian Medical Association v. VP Shantha (1995)
- **Issue**: Whether medical services are "services" under Consumer Protection Act?
- **Held**: Medical services are "services" - Patients are consumers

#### 2. Lucknow Development Authority v. M.K. Gupta (1994)
- **Issue**: Whether housing construction is "service"?
- **Held**: Flat buyers are consumers - Delay in delivery amounts to deficiency

#### 3. Spring Meadows Hospital v. Harjol Ahluwalia (1998)
- **Issue**: Medical negligence - Compensation
- **Held**: Large compensation awarded for medical negligence

#### 4. State Bank of India v. Shri Rajesh Agarwal (2019)
- **Issue**: Banking services deficiency
- **Held**: Compensation for illegal garnishment of account

#### 5. Amitabh Bachchan v. Rajat Nag (2018)
- **Issue**: Celebrity endorser liability for misleading ad
- **Held**: Celebrity can be held liable if misleading ad despite having knowledge

### 3.1.21 Differences: Consumer Protection Act 2019 vs 1986

| Feature | 1986 Act | 2019 Act |
|---------|----------|----------|
| **CCPA** | Not present | Established (Section 10) |
| **Pecuniary Jurisdiction** | District: ₹20 lakh; State: ₹1 crore; National: Above ₹1 crore | District: ₹1 crore; State: ₹10 crore; National: Above ₹10 crore |
| **E-Commerce** | Not specifically covered | Detailed provisions (Section 2(16)) |
| **Product Liability** | Not present | Detailed provisions (Sections 82-87) |
| **Mediation** | Not specifically provided | Mediation cells established (Section 74) |
| **Misleading Advertisement** | Limited provisions | Strong provisions with penalties |
| **Penalties** | Limited | Enhanced penalties |
| **Consumer Rights** | Listed | More elaborated |
| **Direct Selling** | Not covered | Regulated (Section 2(13)) |

### 3.2.1 Quick Summary - RTI Act (For Revision)

#### Objective:
- Transparency and accountability in government
- Right to access information from public authorities
- Based on Article 19(1)(a) - Freedom of speech and expression

#### Key Provisions:
1. **Right**: Every citizen has right to information
2. **Public Authority**: Government bodies, PSUs, NGOs funded by government
3. **Procedure**:
   - Written/electronic application to PIO
   - Fee: Nominal (₹10)
   - Time limit: 30 days (48 hours for life/liberty)
4. **Exemptions**: National security, commercial confidence, personal information (with exceptions)
5. **Appeal**: Two-tier (First Appellate Authority → Information Commission)
6. **Penalty**: ₹250 per day on PIO (max ₹25,000) for non-compliance

#### Business Relevance:
- Access to government policies, tenders, approvals
- Due diligence on government dealings
- Regulatory compliance information
- Competitive intelligence (legitimate)
