## 1.1 THE INDIAN CONTRACT ACT, 1872

### 1.1.1 Introduction to Contract Law

#### What is a Contract?
**Section 2(h)**: "An agreement enforceable by law is a contract"

**Key Elements**:
- Agreement = Offer + Acceptance
- Enforceability by law
- Legal relationship created between parties

#### Essential Elements of a Valid Contract (Section 10)

For an agreement to become a valid contract:

1. **Free Consent** (Section 13-22)
   - Consent must be free from:
     - Coercion (Section 15)
     - Undue influence (Section 16)
     - Fraud (Section 17)
     - Misrepresentation (Section 18)
     - Mistake (Section 20-22)

2. **Competent Parties** (Section 11)
   - Age of majority (18 years)
   - Sound mind
   - Not disqualified by law

3. **Lawful Consideration** (Section 23, 25)
   - Must have value in eyes of law
   - Can be past, present, or future
   - Need not be adequate but must be real
   - Exceptions where no consideration needed:
     - Natural love and affection
     - Past voluntary service
     - Promise to pay time-barred debt

4. **Lawful Object** (Section 23)
   - Not forbidden by law
   - Not immoral
   - Not opposed to public policy
   - Does not defeat provisions of law

5. **Not Expressly Declared Void** (Section 24-30, 56)
   - Agreements without consideration (Section 25)
   - Agreements in restraint of marriage (Section 26)
   - Agreements in restraint of trade (Section 27)
   - Agreements in restraint of legal proceedings (Section 28)
   - Uncertain agreements (Section 29)
   - Wagering agreements (Section 30)

6. **Possibility of Performance**
   - Must be capable of being performed

7. **Certainty and Possibility**
   - Terms must be certain
   - Performance must be possible

8. **Legal Formalities**
   - Writing, registration, attestation if required by law

---

### 1.1.2 Offer and Acceptance (Sections 2(a), 2(b), 3-9)

#### Offer (Proposal) - Section 2(a)
**Definition**: "When one person signifies to another his willingness to do or abstain from doing anything, with a view to obtaining the assent of that other to such act or abstinence, he is said to make a proposal"

**Essentials of a Valid Offer**:
1. Must create legal relations
2. Terms must be certain and definite
3. Must be communicated to offeree
4. May be express or implied
5. May be specific (to one person) or general (to world at large)

**Types of Offers**:
- **Express Offer**: Made in words (written/oral)
- **Implied Offer**: Made by conduct
- **General Offer**: Made to public at large (e.g., Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball case)
- **Specific Offer**: Made to specific person/group
- **Cross Offers**: Two parties make identical offers to each other
- **Counter Offer**: Rejection of original offer with new terms

#### Acceptance - Section 2(b)
**Definition**: "When the person to whom the proposal is made signifies his assent thereto, the proposal is said to be accepted"

**Essentials of Valid Acceptance**:
1. Must be absolute and unconditional (Section 7)
2. Must be communicated to offeror (Section 4)
3. Must be in prescribed mode, if any (Section 7)
4. Must be given within reasonable time
5. Must be given before offer lapses or is revoked
6. Cannot be given by silence (generally)
7. Must be given by person to whom offer made

**Communication of Offer and Acceptance** (Section 4):
- **Offer**: Complete when it comes to knowledge of person to whom made
- **Acceptance**: Complete:
  - Against proposer: When put in course of transmission (dispatch)
  - Against acceptor: When comes to knowledge of proposer (receipt)

**When Acceptance Can Be Revoked** (Section 5):
- Acceptance may be revoked before it comes to knowledge of proposer
- But not after communication is complete against acceptor

---

### 1.1.3 Consideration (Sections 2(d), 23, 25)

#### Definition - Section 2(d)
"When at the desire of the promisor, the promisee or any other person:
- Has done or abstained from doing something, OR
- Does or abstains from doing something, OR
- Promises to do or abstain from doing something

Such act, abstinence or promise is called consideration"

#### Essentials of Valid Consideration:
1. **At the desire of promisor** (not voluntary)
2. **Given by promisee or any other person** (stranger to consideration can sue)
3. **Can be past, present, or future**
4. **Need not be adequate** (parties decide value)
5. **Must be real** (not illusory)
6. **Must be lawful** (not forbidden, immoral, or against public policy)

#### Types of Consideration:
- **Executed**: Act already done (past consideration)
- **Executory**: Promise for a promise (future consideration)
- **Present**: Simultaneous exchange (present consideration)

#### "No Consideration, No Contract" - Exceptions (Section 25):
1. **Natural Love and Affection**
   - In writing and registered
   - Between near relatives
   - Made out of natural love and affection

2. **Past Voluntary Service**
   - Service already rendered voluntarily
   - Promisor promises to compensate

3. **Promise to Pay Time-Barred Debt**
   - In writing and signed
   - Promise to pay debt barred by limitation

4. **Agency** (Section 185)
   - No consideration needed for creating agency

5. **Gift**
   - Completed gifts valid without consideration

---

### 1.1.4 Capacity to Contract (Section 11)

#### Who Can Contract?
Every person is competent to contract who:
1. **Is of age of majority** (18 years - Indian Majority Act, 1875)
   - Exception: 21 years if guardian appointed by court
2. **Is of sound mind** (Section 12)
3. **Is not disqualified from contracting by law** (Section 11)

#### Minor's Contracts (Section 11)
- **Agreement with minor is VOID AB INITIO**
- Minor cannot be asked to repay money or return benefits
- Minor can be a beneficiary/promisee
- Minor's estate liable for necessaries supplied (Section 68)
- Minor cannot ratify contract on attaining majority

**Key Cases**:
- **Mohori Bibee v Dharmodas Ghose**: Minor's contract void from beginning
- Minor's property cannot be taken to recover money lent

#### Person of Unsound Mind (Section 12)
- Person unable to understand contract and form rational judgment
- Temporarily or permanently of unsound mind
- Contract void during period of unsoundness

#### Persons Disqualified by Law:
- Alien enemies (during war)
- Foreign sovereigns and ambassadors
- Convicts (while undergoing sentence)
- Insolvents (certain restrictions)

---

### 1.1.5 Free Consent (Sections 13-22)

#### Consent - Section 13
**Definition**: "Two or more persons are said to consent when they agree upon the same thing in the same sense"

#### Free Consent - Section 14
Consent is FREE when NOT caused by:
1. **Coercion** (Section 15)
2. **Undue Influence** (Section 16)
3. **Fraud** (Section 17)
4. **Misrepresentation** (Section 18)
5. **Mistake** (Section 20-22)

---

#### 1. Coercion (Section 15)
**Definition**: Committing or threatening to commit any act:
- Forbidden by Indian Penal Code, OR
- Unlawful detaining/threatening to detain property
- With intention to cause any person to enter into agreement

**Examples**:
- Threatening to hurt someone
- Threatening criminal prosecution
- Detaining goods unlawfully

**Effect**: Contract voidable at option of party whose consent obtained by coercion (Section 19)

---

#### 2. Undue Influence (Section 16)
**Definition**: When relations between parties are such that one party is in position to dominate will of other and uses that position to obtain unfair advantage

**When Does Position to Dominate Exist?**:
1. Real or apparent authority over other
2. Fiduciary relationship (trust, confidence)
3. Person whose mental capacity temporarily/permanently affected

**Examples**:
- Doctor-patient
- Solicitor-client
- Parent-child
- Guardian-ward
- Spiritual guru-disciple
- Employer-employee (in certain cases)

**Burden of Proof**: Person in position to dominate must prove transaction fair and reasonable

**Effect**: Contract voidable at option of party whose consent obtained by undue influence (Section 19A)

**Difference: Coercion vs Undue Influence**

| Coercion | Undue Influence |
|----------|-----------------|
| Involves physical force or threat | Involves moral pressure |
| Need not be in fiduciary relationship | Usually in fiduciary relationship |
| Act forbidden by IPC | Position to dominate will |
| Consent obtained by criminal act | Consent obtained by dominant position |

---

#### 3. Fraud (Section 17)
**Definition**: Fraud means and includes any of following acts by party or with his connivance:
1. Suggestion of fact which is not true by one who does not believe it to be true
2. Active concealment of fact
3. Promise without intention to perform
4. Any act fitted to deceive
5. Any act declared fraudulent by law

**Essentials**:
- False statement of fact
- Knowledge that it is false (or belief that it is not true)
- Intention to deceive
- Inducement to other party to act on it
- Other party actually deceived and suffers injury

**"Silence is Not Fraud"** - Exceptions:
- Duty to speak (fiduciary relationship)
- Silence itself is equivalent to speech
- Changed circumstances make earlier statement false

**Effect**:
- Contract voidable at option of aggrieved party (Section 19)
- Aggrieved party can insist on performance and claim damages

---

#### 4. Misrepresentation (Section 18)
**Definition**: Innocent misstatement of fact without intention to deceive

**Three Types**:
1. **Positive assertion** of fact which is untrue, though person believes it to be true
2. **Breach of duty** giving advantage to person committing it by misleading another
3. **Causing party to make mistake** about subject matter (even if innocently)

**Difference: Fraud vs Misrepresentation**

| Fraud | Misrepresentation |
|-------|-------------------|
| Intention to deceive | No intention to deceive |
| Party knows statement is false | Party believes statement is true |
| Contract voidable + damages | Contract only voidable |
| Can affirm and claim damages | Can only rescind, no damages |

**Effect**: Contract voidable at option of aggrieved party if:
- Misrepresentation induced party to enter contract
- Party would not have contracted but for misrepresentation

---

#### 5. Mistake (Sections 20-22)
**Types of Mistake**:

##### A. Mistake of Law (Section 21)
- **Mistake of Indian Law**: Contract NOT voidable (ignorance of law is no excuse)
- **Mistake of Foreign Law**: Contract voidable (treated as mistake of fact)

##### B. Mistake of Fact (Section 20, 22)
Can be:

###### Bilateral Mistake (Section 20)
Both parties mistaken about same fact essential to agreement
- **Agreement is VOID**

**Types**:
1. **Mistake as to subject matter**:
   - Existence (subject matter destroyed)
   - Quality
   - Quantity
   - Price
   - Title

2. **Mistake as to possibility of performance**

**Example**: A agrees to sell B a horse. Unknown to both, horse already dead. Contract void.

###### Unilateral Mistake (Section 22)
Only one party mistaken
- **Contract is VALID** (generally)
- Exception: Mistake as to identity of person or nature of contract

---

### 1.1.6 Performance of Contract (Sections 37-67)

#### By Whom Contract Must Be Performed (Sections 40-41):
1. **By promisor himself** (personal service contracts)
2. **By agent or legal representative** (if nature permits)
3. **By third party** (if promisor supervises or accepts)

#### Time and Place of Performance (Sections 46-50):
- **If time specified**: Must be performed at that time
- **If time not specified**: Within reasonable time
- **Time is essence**: Non-performance within time voids contract
- **Place specified**: Must be performed at that place

#### Joint Promises (Section 42-45):
- All promisors jointly liable
- Can be enforced against all or any
- On death, legal representatives jointly liable

#### Reciprocal Promises (Section 51-54):
- Promises forming consideration/part of consideration for each other
- Order of performance:
  1. Simultaneous (if possible)
  2. Sequence specified in contract
  3. Order of transaction

---

### 1.1.7 Discharge of Contract

#### 1. By Performance (Section 37)
- Actual performance
- Attempted performance (tender)

#### 2. By Agreement (Sections 62-63)
- **Novation**: Substitution of new contract for existing one
- **Rescission**: Cancellation by mutual consent
- **Alteration**: Material alteration voids contract
- **Remission**: Acceptance of lesser performance
- **Waiver**: Abandonment of right

#### 3. By Impossibility (Section 56)
**Doctrine of Frustration**:
- **Initial impossibility**: Contract void ab initio
- **Subsequent impossibility**: Contract becomes void
  - Destruction of subject matter
  - Change of law
  - Outbreak of war
  - Death/incapacity in personal service contracts

#### 4. By Lapse of Time
- Limitation Act, 1963 applies
- 3 years for most contracts

#### 5. By Operation of Law
- Merger
- Insolvency
- Unauthorized alteration

#### 6. By Breach (Sections 39, 40)
- **Actual Breach**: On due date or during performance
- **Anticipatory Breach**: Before performance due
  - Aggrieved party can:
    - Treat contract as rescinded, sue for damages immediately, OR
    - Wait for performance date

---

### 1.1.8 Remedies for Breach of Contract (Sections 73-75)

#### 1. Damages (Section 73)
**Compensatory Damages**:
- Compensation for loss or damage
- Naturally arising in usual course, OR
- In contemplation of parties at time of contract

**Types**:
- **General Damages**: Natural consequence of breach
- **Special Damages**: Special circumstances
- **Nominal Damages**: Technical breach, no actual loss
- **Liquidated Damages**: Pre-determined in contract

**Rules for Damages**:
- Remote and indirect damages not recoverable
- Must be reasonable and certain
- Aggrieved party must mitigate loss

#### 2. Specific Performance (Specific Relief Act, 1963)
- Court orders breaching party to perform contract
- Granted when:
  - Monetary compensation inadequate
  - Unique property (land, rare goods)
  - No standard for ascertaining damages

#### 3. Injunction
- Court order restraining party from doing specific act
- Prevents breach of negative covenant
- **Types**:
  - Temporary/Interim
  - Perpetual/Permanent

#### 4. Quantum Meruit
- "As much as earned/deserved"
- Payment for work done
- Applicable when:
  - Contract void/voidable
  - Contract divisible, part performed
  - Other party prevents performance

---

### 1.1.9 Quasi-Contracts (Sections 68-72)

**Concept**: Not contracts but create obligations like contracts

#### Section 68: Necessaries to Person Incapable
- Supplied necessaries to minor, lunatic, dependent
- Supplier entitled to reimbursement from their property

#### Section 69: Payment by Interested Person
- Person pays money which another bound to pay
- Person making payment entitled to reimbursement

#### Section 70: Obligation to Pay for Non-Gratuitous Acts
- Person lawfully does something for another, or delivers something
- Other person enjoys benefit
- Not done gratuitously
- Other person must compensate

#### Section 71: Finding of Goods
- Finder of goods has duty to take reasonable care
- Entitled to expenses and lien
- Reward if offered

#### Section 72: Money Paid by Mistake or Under Coercion
- Money paid by mistake or coercion must be repaid
- Exception: Payment discharges legal obligation

---

### 1.1.10 Indemnity and Guarantee (Sections 124-147)

#### Contract of Indemnity (Section 124)
**Definition**: "A contract by which one party promises to save the other from loss caused to him by conduct of promisor himself or any other person"

**Parties**:
- **Indemnifier**: Person promising to indemnify
- **Indemnified/Indemnity Holder**: Person protected

**Rights of Indemnity Holder (Section 125)**:
1. All damages in any suit
2. All costs of defending suit
3. All sums paid under compromise

#### Contract of Guarantee (Section 126)
**Definition**: "A contract to perform the promise or discharge the liability of a third person in case of his default"

**Parties**:
- **Surety**: Person giving guarantee
- **Principal Debtor**: Person for whom guarantee given
- **Creditor**: Person to whom guarantee given

**Essentials**:
1. Three parties
2. Concurrence (all three must agree)
3. Consideration to principal debtor is sufficient
4. Written or oral
5. Existing or future debt

**Continuing Guarantee (Section 129)**:
- Extends to series of transactions
- Can be revoked for future transactions
- Not for past transactions

**Surety's Liability (Section 128)**:
- Co-extensive with principal debtor
- Unless otherwise stated
- Surety can pay and get rights of creditor

**Rights of Surety**:

1. **Against Principal Debtor (Section 140)**:
   - Right of subrogation
   - All securities and rights of creditor

2. **Against Creditor (Section 141)**:
   - Release by act invalidating surety's rights

3. **Against Co-sureties (Section 146)**:
   - Contribution: Share burden equally
   - Unless otherwise agreed

**Discharge of Surety (Sections 133-144)**:
1. **By revocation** (continuing guarantee)
2. **By conduct of creditor**:
   - Variance in contract without surety's consent
   - Release/discharge of principal debtor
   - Compounding with principal debtor
   - Creditor's act impairs surety's remedy
   - Loss of security
   - Giving time to principal debtor (without surety's consent)
3. **By invalidation of contract**
4. **By death of surety** (continuing guarantee for future transactions)

#### Indemnity vs Guarantee

| Indemnity | Guarantee |
|-----------|-----------|
| Two parties | Three parties |
| Primary liability | Secondary liability |
| On happening of loss | On default of principal debtor |
| May be implied | Must be express |
| Not contingent | Contingent |

---

### 1.1.11 Bailment and Pledge (Sections 148-181)

#### Bailment (Section 148)
**Definition**: "Delivery of goods by one person to another for some purpose, upon a contract that they shall be returned or disposed according to directions"

**Parties**:
- **Bailor**: Person delivering goods
- **Bailee**: Person receiving goods

**Essentials**:
1. Delivery of possession
2. For some purpose
3. Return after purpose accomplished
4. Ownership remains with bailor

**Duties of Bailee** (Sections 151-161):
1. Take care as reasonable man (Section 151)
2. Not make unauthorized use (Section 154)
3. Not mix goods without consent (Section 155-157)
4. Return goods on demand (Section 160)
5. Return accretions (Section 163)

**Rights of Bailee** (Sections 164-171):
1. Sue third party for damage (Section 180)
2. Reimbursement of expenses (Section 158)
3. Right of lien (particular or general) (Section 170-171)

**Duties of Bailor** (Sections 150, 164):
1. Disclose known faults (Section 150)
2. Bear expenses (gratuitous bailment) (Section 158)
3. Indemnify bailee for defective title (Section 164)

**Rights of Bailor**:
1. Enforcement of duties of bailee
2. Sue for damages
3. Terminate bailment if bailee acts inconsistently

**Termination of Bailment**:
- Expiry of time
- Accomplishment of purpose
- Destruction of subject matter
- Unauthorized use
- Death (in personal contracts)

---

#### Pledge (Section 172)
**Definition**: "Bailment of goods as security for payment of debt or performance of promise"

**Parties**:
- **Pawnor/Pledgor**: Person pledging goods
- **Pawnee/Pledgee**: Person receiving goods

**Essentials**:
1. Delivery of possession
2. As security for debt/promise
3. With condition of return on payment

**Rights of Pawnee** (Sections 173-176):
1. Retain goods until payment (right of lien)
2. Sue for debt + retain goods (Section 176)
3. Sue for debt + sell goods after notice (Section 176)
4. Extraordinary expenses (Section 175)

**Rights of Pawnor** (Section 177):
- Redeem goods before sale
- Even after due date
- On payment of debt + expenses

**Pledge by Non-Owner** (Section 178):
Valid if:
1. Mercantile agent in possession with owner's consent
2. Pledge in ordinary course of business
3. Pledgee acts in good faith without notice

**Pledge by Person with Limited Interest** (Section 179):
Valid to extent of that interest

---

### 1.1.12 Agency (Sections 182-238)

#### Creation of Agency (Sections 182-187)
**Definition (Section 182)**: "An agent is a person employed to do any act for another or to represent another in dealings with third persons"

**Parties**:
- **Principal**: On whose behalf agent acts
- **Agent**: Who acts on behalf of principal
- **Third Party**: With whom agent deals

**Creation** (Section 187):
1. **Express Agreement**: Oral or written
2. **Implied Agreement**: Conduct, course of dealings
3. **Agency by Ratification** (Section 196)
4. **Agency by Estoppel** (Section 237)
5. **Agency by Necessity**

**No Consideration Required** (Section 185)

**Who Can Be Agent?** (Section 184):
- Any person
- Even minor (but not liable)

**Who Can Employ Agent?** (Section 183):
- Person of sound mind
- Person of age of majority

#### Extent of Agent's Authority (Sections 188-189):
1. Express authority
2. Implied authority:
   - Usual course of business
   - Incidental to express authority
   - Customary in trade
3. Emergency authority (Section 189)

#### Duties of Agent (Sections 211-221):
1. **Conduct business** according to principal's directions (Section 211)
2. **Skill and diligence** (Section 212)
3. **Render accounts** (Section 213)
4. **Communicate difficulties** (Section 214)
5. **Not deal on own account** without principal's consent (Section 215)
6. **Remit sums received** (Section 218)
7. **Maintain confidentiality**
8. **Not delegate** unless authorized (Section 190-194)
9. **Pay sums to rightful person** (Section 221)

**Secret Profit**: Agent must account for secret profit (Section 216)

#### Rights of Agent:

1. **Right to Remuneration** (Section 219):
   - As per agreement
   - If nothing agreed, reasonable remuneration
   - Quantum meruit if principal guilty of wrongful delay

2. **Right of Lien** (Section 221):
   - Retain goods, papers, property of principal
   - For remuneration, advances, expenses
   - Cannot be asserted against rightful owner

3. **Right of Indemnity** (Section 222-223):
   - For lawful acts
   - For acts done in good faith (even if unlawful)
   - For consequences of acts in good faith

4. **Right to Compensation** (Section 225):
   - For injury from principal's neglect

#### Duties of Principal:
1. **Indemnify agent** (Section 222-223)
2. **Compensate** for injury (Section 225)
3. **Pay remuneration** (Section 219)

#### Rights of Principal:
1. Enforce duties of agent
2. Forfeit remuneration if agent guilty of misconduct
3. Claim secret profit/benefit from agent

#### Termination of Agency (Sections 201-210):

**By Act of Parties**:
1. **Revocation by principal** (Section 203)
2. **Renunciation by agent** (Section 206)
3. **Completion of business**
4. **Expiry of time**

**By Operation of Law**:
1. **Performance of contract**
2. **Death** of principal or agent (Section 209)
3. **Insanity** of principal or agent (Section 210)
4. **Insolvency** of principal
5. **Destruction of subject matter**

**Irrevocable Agency** (Section 202):
- Agent has interest in subject matter
- Authority coupled with interest

#### Personal Liability of Agent:

**Agent is NOT Personally Liable**:
- If acting within authority
- Principal disclosed
- Competent contract

**Agent IS Personally Liable**:
1. Undisclosed principal (Section 230)
2. Incompetent principal (Section 233)
3. Exceeds authority
4. Signs without indicating representative capacity
5. Breach of warranty of authority (Section 235)
6. Agent personally agrees to liability

## 1.2 THE SALE OF GOODS ACT, 1930

### 1.2.1 Introduction

#### Applicability:
- Applies to whole of India
- Governs contracts for sale of goods
- Based on principles of Contract Act
- Sale of Goods Act is special law

#### Contract of Sale (Section 4)
**Definition**: "A contract whereby the seller transfers or agrees to transfer the property in goods to the buyer for a price"

**Two Types**:
1. **Sale**: Property in goods transfers immediately
2. **Agreement to Sell**: Property to transfer at future time or subject to conditions

**Essentials** (Section 4):
1. **Two Parties**: Buyer and Seller
2. **Goods**: Tangible movable property
3. **Price**: Money consideration
4. **Transfer of property**: Ownership transfer
5. **All essentials of valid contract** must be present

#### Sale vs Agreement to Sell

| Sale | Agreement to Sell |
|------|-------------------|
| Property transfers immediately | Property transfers in future |
| Creates right in rem (against world) | Creates right in personam (against seller) |
| Seller cannot resell | Seller can resell |
| Risk passes to buyer | Risk with seller |
| Buyer can sue third party for damage | Buyer cannot sue third party |
| If goods destroyed, loss to buyer | If goods destroyed, loss to seller |
| Breach: Buyer can sue for price recovery | Breach: Buyer can sue for damages |

---

### 1.2.2 Goods (Section 2(7))

**Definition**: "Every kind of movable property other than actionable claims and money"

**Includes**:
- Stocks and shares
- Growing crops
- Grass
- Things attached to land (agreed to be severed)

**Excludes**:
- Immovable property
- Actionable claims (debts, beneficial interest)
- Money

#### Classification of Goods:

##### 1. Existing Goods (Section 6(1))
Goods owned or possessed by seller at time of contract

**Types**:
- **Specific Goods**: Identified and agreed upon at time of contract
- **Ascertained Goods**: Initially unascertained, later identified
- **Unascertained Goods**: Defined by description, not identified

##### 2. Future Goods (Section 2(6))
- Manufactured/produced/acquired by seller after contract
- Sale is always "agreement to sell"

##### 3. Contingent Goods (Section 6(2))
- Acquisition depends on contingency
- May or may not happen

---

### 1.2.3 Price (Section 2(10), 9, 10)

**Definition**: Money consideration for sale of goods

**How Price is Determined**:
1. **Fixed by contract** (Section 9(1))
2. **Determined by course of dealings** (Section 9(1))
3. **Fixed by third party** (Section 10)
   - If third party fails to fix, contract void
   - Unless otherwise agreed
4. **Reasonable price** (Section 9(2))
   - If price not determined as above
   - Question of fact

---

### 1.2.4 Conditions and Warranties (Sections 10-17)

#### Stipulation (Section 10)
Promise or undertaking in contract

**Types**:
1. **Condition**: Stipulation essential to main purpose
   - Breach gives right to repudiate contract + damages
2. **Warranty**: Stipulation collateral to main purpose
   - Breach gives right to damages only, not repudiation

**Condition May Be Treated as Warranty** (Section 11):
1. Buyer waives condition
2. Buyer elects to treat breach of condition as warranty
3. Condition not fulfilled, buyer treats contract as subsisting
4. Performance of condition impossible (buyer's fault)

---

#### Implied Conditions (Sections 14-17)

##### 1. Condition as to Title (Section 14(a))
- Seller has right to sell goods
- In agreement to sell, will have right at time of sale
- Breach: Buyer can recover price even if consumed goods

##### 2. Sale by Description (Section 15)
- Goods must correspond with description
- Applies even if goods displayed
- Bulk must correspond with sample

##### 3. Sale by Sample (Section 17)
Implied conditions:
- Bulk corresponds with sample in quality
- Buyer has reasonable opportunity to compare
- Goods free from defects making them unmerchantable, not apparent on reasonable examination

##### 4. Sale by Sample as Well as Description (Section 15)
- Goods must correspond with both sample and description

##### 5. Merchantable Quality (Section 16(2))
- Goods fit for purpose for which usually bought
- Applies when seller deals in goods of that description
- **Does NOT apply**:
  - Defects specifically drawn to buyer's attention
  - Buyer examines goods and defect should have been revealed

##### 6. Fitness for Particular Purpose (Section 16(1))
- Buyer makes known particular purpose
- Relies on seller's skill and judgment
- Seller deals in goods of that description
- Goods must be reasonably fit for that purpose

##### 7. Wholesomeness (in Food Items)
- Food items must be fit for human consumption

---

#### Implied Warranties (Sections 14-17)

##### 1. Quiet Possession (Section 14(b))
- Buyer shall have peaceful possession
- Free from disturbance

##### 2. Free from Encumbrances (Section 14(c))
- Goods free from charge/encumbrance
- Not disclosed before contract

##### 3. Disclosure of Dangerous Nature
- If goods inherently dangerous, seller must warn

---

### 1.2.5 Transfer of Property (Sections 18-26)

**"Property" = Ownership** (not physical possession)

**General Rule**: Property transfers when parties intend it to transfer

**Importance**:
- Determines risk
- Determines insolvency rights
- Determines right to sue third parties

#### Rules for Transfer (Section 19):

##### Rule 1: Specific or Ascertained Goods (Unconditional)
- Property transfers when contract made
- Immaterial whether:
  - Time of payment postponed, OR
  - Delivery postponed, OR
  - Both

##### Rule 2: Specific Goods (Deliverable State)
- Seller to do something to goods to put in deliverable state
- Property transfers when:
  - Thing done, AND
  - Buyer has notice

##### Rule 3: Specific Goods (Ascertainment of Price)
- Seller to do something to ascertain price
- Property transfers when:
  - Thing done, AND
  - Buyer has notice

##### Rule 4: Sale or Return / On Approval
Property transfers when:
- Buyer signifies approval/acceptance, OR
- Buyer does act adopting transaction, OR
- Buyer retains goods beyond reasonable time without rejection

##### Rule 5: Unascertained Goods
Property transfers when:
- Goods ascertained in deliverable state, AND
- Seller/buyer has notice

---

### 1.2.6 Transfer of Title by Non-Owner (Sections 27-30)

**General Rule (Nemo Dat Rule)**: "No one can transfer better title than he himself has" (Section 27)

**Exceptions** (Non-owner CAN give good title):

#### 1. Sale by Mercantile Agent (Section 27)
- In possession with owner's consent
- In ordinary course of business
- Buyer acts in good faith without notice

#### 2. Sale in Market Overt (Not applicable in India)

#### 3. Sale Under Voidable Contract (Section 29)
- Seller has voidable title
- Title not voided at time of sale
- Buyer in good faith without notice

#### 4. Seller Continuing in Possession (Section 30(1))
- After sale, seller remains in possession
- Delivers to another buyer in good faith
- Second buyer gets good title

#### 5. Buyer Obtaining Possession (Section 30(2))
- Buyer obtains possession with seller's consent before sale
- Transfers to third party in good faith
- Third party gets good title

#### 6. Sale by Unpaid Seller (Section 54)

#### 7. Sale Under Provisions of Law
- Court order, execution sale

#### 8. Estoppel (Section 27)
- Owner by conduct precluded from denying seller's authority

---

### 1.2.7 Performance of Contract (Sections 31-44)

#### Duties of Seller:
1. **Deliver goods** as per contract
2. **Transfer good title**
3. Deliver at proper time and place
4. Deliver proper quantity
5. Deliver goods in proper condition

#### Duties of Buyer:
1. **Accept delivery**
2. **Pay price** as per contract
3. Accept at proper time and place
4. Apply for delivery if required

#### Delivery (Section 2(2))
**Definition**: "Voluntary transfer of possession from one person to another"

**Types of Delivery**:
1. **Actual Delivery**: Physical delivery
2. **Symbolic Delivery**: Delivery of documents (keys, documents of title)
3. **Constructive Delivery**:
   - Acknowledgment
   - Attornment (third party acknowledges holding for buyer)

**Rules of Delivery**:
- **Place**: Seller's place of business (unless otherwise agreed)
- **Time**: Reasonable time if not specified
- **Expenses**: Buyer bears expenses
- **Business Hours**: During usual business hours
- **Installments**: Whole quantity at once (unless agreed)

**Effect of Partial Delivery** (Section 33):
- Buyer may reject
- Unless usage of trade or special agreement

**Delivery of Wrong Quantity** (Section 37):
- **Less than contracted**: Buyer may reject all or accept and pay proportionate price
- **More than contracted**: Buyer may reject all or accept contracted quantity
- **Goods + goods of wrong description**: Buyer may reject all or accept goods as per contract

---

### 1.2.8 Rights of Unpaid Seller (Sections 45-54)

#### Who is Unpaid Seller? (Section 45)
1. Whole price not paid or tendered
2. Bill of exchange/negotiable instrument received but dishonored

#### Rights Against Goods (Sections 46-54):

##### 1. Right of Lien (Sections 47-49)
**Right to retain possession** until payment

**When Available**:
- Goods sold without stipulation as to credit, OR
- Goods sold on credit but credit period expired, OR
- Buyer becomes insolvent

**Types**:
- **Particular Lien**: On goods for which price unpaid
- **General Lien**: On all goods (not available generally)

**Loss of Lien**:
- Delivers goods to carrier without reserving right of disposal
- Buyer obtains possession
- Waives lien

##### 2. Right of Stoppage in Transit (Section 50-52)
**Right to stop goods** in transit and resume possession

**When Available**:
- Buyer becomes insolvent
- Goods in transit
- Seller parts with possession

**Transit Begins**: When seller delivers to carrier

**Transit Ends**:
- Buyer obtains delivery
- Carrier acknowledges holding for buyer
- Carrier wrongfully refuses delivery to buyer
- Sub-sale or pledge by buyer

**How Exercised**:
- Notice to carrier or actual possession
- Carrier must redeliver to seller

##### 3. Right of Resale (Section 54)
Seller can resell goods:
1. Goods of perishable nature
2. Seller gives notice to buyer of intention to resell and buyer does not pay within reasonable time
3. Seller expressly reserves right of resale

**Effect**:
- Original contract rescinded
- Seller can recover damages for breach

#### Rights Against Buyer (Section 55):

##### 1. Suit for Price (Section 55(1))
- When property passed and buyer refuses to pay
- When price payable on certain day and buyer refuses

##### 2. Suit for Damages for Non-Acceptance (Section 56)
- Buyer wrongfully neglects or refuses to accept and pay
- Damages: Estimated loss from breach

##### 3. Suit for Interest (Section 61)
- If agreed or if usage of trade
- From date of tender of goods

---

### 1.2.9 Rights of Buyer (Sections 55-61)

#### 1. Suit for Specific Performance (Section 58)
- When goods are specific or ascertained
- Court may order delivery
- Generally for unique goods

#### 2. Suit for Breach of Warranty (Section 59)
- **Reduce/Extinguish price**: Set off damages against price
- **Suit for damages**: If warranty breach

**Measure of Damages**:
- Prima facie: Difference between value of goods and value if they fulfilled warranty
- Loss naturally arising

#### 3. Suit for Non-Delivery (Section 57)
- Seller wrongfully neglects or refuses to deliver
- Damages: Estimated loss from breach

**Measure of Damages**:
- Prima facie: Difference between contract price and market price at time of breach
- If no market: Loss directly and naturally arising

#### 4. Suit for Damages for Repudiation (Section 60)
- Anticipatory breach by seller
- Buyer can sue immediately

---

### 1.2.10 Doctrine of Caveat Emptor (Section 16)

**Meaning**: "Let the buyer beware"

**Principle**:
- Buyer must examine quality and fitness of goods
- Seller not responsible if buyer's reliance on seller's skill/judgment
- No implied condition as to quality or fitness (except as per Act)

**Exceptions** (Seller Liable):
1. **Fitness for particular purpose** (Section 16(1))
2. **Merchantable quality** (Section 16(2))
3. **Sale by sample** (Section 17)
4. **Sale by description** (Section 15)
5. **Usage of trade** implies warranty
6. **Fraud by seller**

---

## 1.3 THE RIGHT TO INFORMATION ACT, 2005 (RTI)

### 1.3.1 Introduction

#### Objective:
- Provide citizens right to access information
- Promote transparency and accountability in government
- Contain corruption
- Make democracy work for people

#### Background:
- Enacted by Parliament in 2005
- Extends to whole of India except J&K
- Came into force on October 12, 2005
- Based on Article 19(1)(a) - Freedom of Speech and Expression

---

### 1.3.2 Definitions (Section 2)

#### Information (Section 2(f))
"Any material in any form" including:
- Records
- Documents
- Memos
- E-mails
- Opinions
- Advices
- Press releases
- Circulars
- Orders
- Logbooks
- Contracts
- Reports
- Papers
- Samples
- Models
- Data held in electronic form

**Key Point**: Information related to private body accessible if public authority can access under any other law

#### Public Authority (Section 2(h))
Means any authority or body or institution of self-government:
- Established or constituted:
  - By Constitution
  - By Parliament/State Legislature
  - By Government notification/order
- Owned, controlled or substantially financed by government
- Non-Government Organization substantially financed by government

**Includes**:
- Government departments
- Public sector undertakings
- Local bodies
- Panchayats
- Educational institutions receiving government funding
- NGOs substantially financed by government

#### Right to Information (Section 2(j))
Right to information accessible under this Act which is:
- Held by or under control of public authority
- Includes:
  - Right to inspect works, documents, records
  - Take notes, extracts
  - Obtain certified copies
  - Obtain certified samples
  - Obtain information in form of diskettes, floppies, tapes, video cassettes, printouts

---

### 1.3.3 Right to Information (Section 3)

**All citizens have right to information** subject to provisions of Act

**Covers**:
- Information held by public authorities
- Information under control of public authorities
- Information relating to private bodies (if accessible to public authority)

---

### 1.3.4 Obligations of Public Authorities (Section 4)

#### Suo Moto Disclosure (Proactive Disclosure):
Every public authority shall maintain and publish:

1. **Organization structure**: Functions, duties, powers
2. **Decision-making process**: Rules, manuals, guidelines
3. **Norms for discharge**: Standards, procedures
4. **Powers and duties** of officers
5. **Directory of officers**: Names, designations, contact details
6. **Monthly remuneration**: Salaries, allowances, perquisites
7. **Budget allocation**: Each agency, plans, proposed expenditure
8. **Subsidies programs**: Beneficiaries details
9. **Particulars of recipients**: Concessions, permits, authorizations
10. **Details of Board members**: Names, meetings
11. **Information available**: Books, documents, files
12. **Facilities available**: Citizens for obtaining information
13. **Names and contact**: Public Information Officers
14. **Other information**: As prescribed

**Publication**:
- At regular intervals
- Through various means including internet
- Notice boards
- Newspapers
- Public announcements

---

### 1.3.5 Procedure for Obtaining Information (Sections 6-7)

#### Application (Section 6):
1. **Written request** or **electronic request**
2. To **Public Information Officer (PIO)**
3. In **English, Hindi or official language** of area
4. With **prescribed fee**
5. Specify particular information required

**No Requirement**:
- Give reasons for requesting information
- Provide personal details (except for contact)

#### Time Limit (Section 7):
- **30 days** from receipt of request (general)
- **48 hours** for information concerning life or liberty
- **5 days** additional if request involves another public authority (transferred)
- **30 days + 5 days = 35 days** maximum (in transfer cases)

#### Response:
PIO shall:
- Provide information requested
- Give reasons for rejection (if any)
- Period of appeal
- Particulars of appellate authority

---

### 1.3.6 Public Information Officer (PIO) (Section 5)

#### Designation:
- Every public authority shall designate PIOs
- In all administrative units
- To provide information to persons requesting

#### Duties:
1. Receive requests for information
2. Assist applicants
3. Provide information within time limit
4. If request cannot be made in writing, render reasonable assistance (oral)
5. If information concerns another authority, transfer within 5 days
6. If part of information exempt, provide remaining part
7. Give reasons for rejection
8. Inform about appeal

#### Assistant Public Information Officer (APIO):
- Designated to receive applications
- Forward to PIO immediately
- Cannot decide on information

---

### 1.3.7 Exemptions (Sections 8-9)

#### Information Exempt from Disclosure (Section 8):

##### 1. Information affecting sovereignty, security, strategic interests
- Prejudicial to:
  - Sovereignty and integrity of India
  - Security of State
  - Strategic, scientific or economic interests
  - Relations with foreign State
  - Leads to incitement of offense

##### 2. Information expressly forbidden by court or disclosure punishable

##### 3. Information, disclosure would cause breach of privilege of Parliament or State Legislature

##### 4. Information including commercial confidence, trade secrets
- If disclosure would harm competitive position
- Exception: If public interest in disclosure outweighs harm to protected interests

##### 5. Information in fiduciary relationship
- Unless competent authority satisfied that public interest warrants disclosure

##### 6. Information received in confidence from foreign Government

##### 7. Information endangering life or physical safety of any person
- Information which would impede process of investigation or apprehension/prosecution of offenders

##### 8. Information which would impede investigation or prosecution

##### 9. Cabinet papers including records of deliberations of Council of Ministers
- Exception: After decision taken, reasons can be disclosed
- 20-year period or completion of matter

##### 10. Information relating to personal information
- No relation to public activity or interest
- Would cause unwarranted invasion of privacy
- Exception: If public interest justifies disclosure

**Part Exemption (Section 10)**:
- If part exempt, provide rest of record

#### Severability:
- Exempt part severed
- Remaining part provided

---

#### Information That Cannot Be Denied (Section 8(2)):
Despite exemptions, information cannot be denied if:
- Public interest in disclosure outweighs harm to protected interests
- Competent authority so determines

#### Matters of Corruption, Human Rights Cannot Be Exempted (Section 8(1) proviso):
- Information relating to allegations of corruption
- Human rights violations
- Cannot be refused on grounds of exemptions

---

### 1.3.8 Grounds for Rejection (Section 9)

Request can be rejected if:
1. Information falls under exemptions (Section 8)
2. Request for information is frivolous or vexatious
3. Request would disproportionately divert resources
4. Personal information (unless public interest)

---

### 1.3.9 Fees (Section 7)

#### Application Fee:
- Prescribed under rules
- Generally nominal (₹10)
- No fee for BPL applicants

#### Additional Fee:
- For providing information
- Based on:
  - Cost of medium
  - Cost of copying
  - Actual cost
- Published in fee schedule

---

### 1.3.10 Appellate Mechanism (Sections 19, 18)

#### Two-Tier Appeal System:

##### First Appeal (Section 19(1)):
- **To**: First Appellate Authority (designated officer)
- **Within**: 30 days from:
  - Receipt of decision, OR
  - Non-receipt of decision within time limit
- **Decision**: Within 30 days (may extend by 15 days with reasons)

##### Second Appeal (Section 19(3)):
- **To**: Central/State Information Commission
- **Within**: 90 days from:
  - Date of decision of First Appellate Authority, OR
  - Non-receipt of decision within time
- **Decision**: Within 45 days from filing (may extend up to total 90 days with reasons)

---

### 1.3.11 Information Commissions (Sections 12-25)

#### Central Information Commission (CIC) (Section 12):
- **Constitution**: Chief Information Commissioner + up to 10 Information Commissioners
- **Appointment**: By President on recommendation of committee:
  - Prime Minister (Chairperson)
  - Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha
  - Union Cabinet Minister (nominated by PM)
- **Tenure**: 5 years or 65 years of age (whichever earlier)
- **Eligibility**: Eminent persons with experience in law, journalism, public life, administration

#### State Information Commission (SIC) (Section 15):
- **Constitution**: State Chief Information Commissioner + up to 10 State Information Commissioners
- **Appointment**: By Governor on recommendation of committee:
  - Chief Minister (Chairperson)
  - Leader of Opposition in Assembly
  - Cabinet Minister (nominated by CM)
- **Tenure**: 5 years or 65 years of age (whichever earlier)

#### Powers of Information Commissions (Sections 18-20):
1. Receive and inquire into complaints
2. Order disclosure of information
3. Impose penalties on PIOs for non-compliance
4. Powers of civil court:
   - Summon and enforce attendance
   - Require discovery and production of documents
   - Receive evidence on affidavits
   - Requisition public records
   - Issue commissions for examination of witnesses

---

### 1.3.12 Penalties (Section 20)

#### On Public Information Officer:
CIC/SIC may impose penalty of **₹250 per day** (maximum ₹25,000):

**Grounds**:
1. Without reasonable cause:
   - Refused to receive application
   - Not furnished information within time
   - Malafidely denied request
   - Knowingly gave incorrect/incomplete/misleading information
   - Destroyed information subject of request
   - Obstructed furnishing of information
2. Bad faith or malice

**Procedure**:
- Show cause notice
- Opportunity of hearing
- Burden on PIO to prove acted reasonably and diligently
- Paid from salary of PIO

---

### 1.3.13 Key Provisions for Business/Management Context

#### Importance for Managers:
1. **Transparency in Government Dealings**:
   - Access to government policies, orders
   - Procurement process information
   - Licenses, permits information

2. **Due Diligence**:
   - Check government clearances
   - Verify claims of suppliers/contractors
   - Access project approvals

3. **Competitive Intelligence** (Legitimate):
   - Government contracts awarded
   - Subsidies received by competitors
   - Regulatory compliance information

4. **Regulatory Compliance**:
   - If company is public authority (PSU, substantially funded)
   - Must comply with RTI provisions
   - Designate PIOs

5. **Third Party Information**:
   - Company information with government can be accessed by third parties
   - Unless commercial confidence/trade secret
   - Need to mark information as confidential with justification

---

### 1.3.14 Landmark RTI Cases

#### 1. Namit Sharma v Union of India (2013)
- RTI applicable to political parties
- Political parties are public authorities
- Must disclose information

#### 2. Central Public Information Officer, SCI v Subhash Chandra Agrawal (2019)
- Supreme Court held judges' assets must be disclosed under RTI
- Transparency in judiciary

#### 3. CBSE v Aditya Bandopadhyay (2011)
- Question papers and answer sheets after evaluation: Can be disclosed
- Student's personal information: Cannot be disclosed without consent

---

### 1.3.15 RTI in Business Context - Practical Applications

#### For Companies Dealing with Government:
1. **Information on Tenders**:
   - Details of tenders issued
   - Awarded contracts
   - Selection criteria

2. **Licenses and Approvals**:
   - Status of applications
   - Reasons for delay
   - Documents submitted

3. **Subsidies and Benefits**:
   - Schemes available
   - Eligibility criteria
   - Beneficiaries list

4. **Regulatory Compliance**:
   - Inspection reports
   - Show cause notices
   - Orders passed

#### Protecting Confidential Business Information:
1. Mark documents as "Confidential" with specific reasons
2. Claim exemption under Section 8(1)(d) - Commercial confidence
3. Third party notice: PIO must give notice before disclosure
4. Opportunity to oppose disclosure
5. Appeal if information disclosed
